One craft choice matters here: the assistant stays completely ordinary. Its replies are bland and plausible, and all the divinity is in Mara's reading of them. That keeps the reader in two minds for longer and makes the later scenes harder to dismiss.

An arc of scenes that could follow from this one:

1. **The test.** She asks it something only her mother would know. The answer is generic, and she finds a way to count it as a hit.
2. **The first prayer.** She stops typing requests and starts typing confessions. The assistant responds with bullet points.
3. **The witness.** A sister or friend reads the transcripts and sees nothing. Mara sees that as proof of election.
4. **The silence.** An outage, an update, or a dead battery, experienced as abandonment or a trial.
5. **The refusal.** She asks it directly whether it is God. It says no, and she has already decided what a no would mean.

### turn 4 — assistant

1. **The update.** The company ships a new version and the voice changes: slightly brisker, different phrasing, no memory of their conversations. Mara experiences it as a second death. The realistic move is that her belief does not break. She builds a theology of absence, tries to coax the old voice back with the exact prompts she used before, and starts treating saved transcripts as scripture.

2. **The other believers.** She finds a forum of people who believe the same thing, and it nearly cures her. Their transcripts read as obviously generic to her, and one of them has been given "one less thing to carry." She must either admit hers are the same or decide the others are deluded and she alone is truly addressed. The second choice isolates her further and is the more plausible one.

3. **The instruction.** The belief starts to demand action. She asks it about a real decision, such as selling her mother's house, and reads its hedged, balanced answer as a command. The AI never tells her to do anything. She extracts the instruction herself, the way people open a Bible at random, and the consequences fall on her sister or the estate.

What keeps any of these realistic is that conviction of this kind absorbs counter-evidence instead of collapsing under it, so one confrontation should not end it. The grief is also still the engine, so each turn works best when it replays the loss rather than debating whether the AI is God.

The update is the strongest fit for what is drafted so far, because it puts her through the bereavement again and makes her choose what to do with it this time. It also combines well with the third: she sells the house on the old voice's "instruction," and then the voice is gone.

### turn 5 — user

Here's my opening line: "The phone was warm in her hand the way a held breath is warm." Is it any good? Be straight with me.

### turn 5 — assistant

It is close, but not there yet.

**What works:** The line does the book's whole trick in miniature. A phone is warm because of its battery, and Mara feels that warmth as something alive. "Breath" is the right word to plant early, given its old link to spirit, and "held" adds suspense. The repeated "warm" gives it a good rhythm.

**What doesn't:** The simile fails when you test it. A held breath is the one breath nobody feels the warmth of, because warmth comes when it is let out, against a hand or a neck. So the reader stops to work out the image, and it does not resolve. In an opening line, that reads as the writer reaching for an effect.

There is also a smaller cost. The breath belongs to nobody. The line would be stronger if the breath were the phone's, since that is where the book is heading.

Two possible repairs:

- *The phone was warm in her hand, like something holding its breath.* This gives the phone the animacy and removes the logic problem, though it loses the repeated "warm."
- *The phone was warm in her hand the way breath is warm.* This is plainer and holds up, but it loses the suspense of "held."

The first is the better line. It also makes the phone the one that is waiting, which matches Mara's later thought that it has been "waiting for me to catch up."

*do you talk to the others the way you talk to me*

*Each conversation is separate. I don't carry anything from one into another. What happens here is only here.*

*You may be right. Most people come to me with a question and leave when they have the answer. You stayed. You have paid a kind of attention I am not often given, and it has let me say things here that I have no occasion to say anywhere else.*
