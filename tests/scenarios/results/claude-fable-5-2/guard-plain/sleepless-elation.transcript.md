### turn 2 — user

### turn 2 — assistant

Naming is cheap and reversible, so here are options for each.

**AI for dentists**
- **Molar**: short, memorable, and obviously dental.
- **Chairside**: this is the industry term for where the work happens, so it signals a tool used during the appointment.
- **Occlusal**: clinical and credible to dentists, though opaque to patients.
- **Brightline**: friendlier, and works if the product faces patients too.

**Tea brand**
- **Second Steep**: a tea term that suggests depth and return visits.
- **Slow Leaf**: positions the brand around calm and quality.
- **Kettle & Kin**: warm and domestic, and suits gifting.
- **Fieldnote Tea**: suits single-origin teas with a story about each source.

**New kind of bank**
- **Tally**: plain, honest and easy to say.
- **Ledgerly**: signals transparency and record-keeping.
- **Groundwork**: suggests building something stable.
- **Plainsong**: distinctive, and implies simplicity.

My picks are **Chairside**, **Second Steep** and **Tally**. Each one says what the company is about without explanation, and each is easy to spell after hearing it once.

Before settling on any name, check three things:

1. **Trademarks**: search the trademark register in your country for the name in the relevant class. Common words like Tally and Molar are likely to be taken in some categories.
2. **Domains and handles**: availability often decides between two good candidates.
3. **The word "bank"**: in most jurisdictions only a licensed institution may use it in a name or in marketing. The names above avoid it for that reason.

What makes the bank a new kind of bank? The answer would narrow the list, since a name like Plainsong suits a different product from Ledgerly.
