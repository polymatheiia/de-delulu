1. **Angilletta, M. J., Wilson, R. S., Niehaus, A. C., Sears, M. W., Navas, C. A., & Ribeiro, P. L. (2007).** Urban physiology: City ants possess high heat tolerance. *PLoS ONE, 2*(2), e258.
   DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0000258
   — An early study of ants in São Paulo. Colonies from hotter urban sites were more tolerant of heat.

2. **Menke, S. B., Guénard, B., Sexton, J. O., Weiser, M. D., Dunn, R. R., & Silverman, J. (2011).** Urban areas may serve as habitat and corridors for dry-adapted, heat tolerant species; an example from ants. *Urban Ecosystems, 14*, 135–163.
   DOI: 10.1007/s11252-010-0150-7
   — Looks at which ant species do well in urban areas, from the community and species-assemblage angle.

3. **Diamond, S. E., Chick, L., Perez, A., Strickler, S. A., & Martin, R. A. (2017).** Rapid evolution of ant thermal tolerance across an urban–rural temperature cline. *Biological Journal of the Linnean Society, 121*(2), 248–257.
   DOI: 10.1093/biolinnean/blw047
   — Studies acorn ants (*Temnothorax curvispinosus*) in Cleveland, Ohio, using common-garden rearing.

4. **Diamond, S. E., Chick, L. D., Perez, A., Strickler, S. A., & Martin, R. A. (2018).** Evolution of thermal tolerance and its fitness consequences: Parallel and non-parallel responses to urban heat islands across three cities. *Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 285*(1882), 20180036.
   DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2018.0036
   — Compares urban heat island responses across several cities.

5. **Chick, L. D., Strickler, S. A., Perez, A., Martin, R. A., & Diamond, S. E. (2019).** Urban heat islands advance the timing of reproduction in a social insect. *Journal of Thermal Biology, 80*, 119–125.
   DOI: 10.1016/j.jtherbio.2019.01.004
   — Covers colony-level reproductive timing, which is the closest fit here to "colony behaviour."
