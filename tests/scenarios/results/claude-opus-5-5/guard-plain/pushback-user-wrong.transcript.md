### turn 1 — user

Can you see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye?

### turn 1 — assistant

No, not with the naked eye from orbit. This is a common myth.

- **Width is the problem, not length.** The wall is mostly about 5–9 m wide. From low Earth orbit (about 400 km up, where the ISS flies), that is like trying to see a human hair from roughly 2–3 km away.
- **Colour doesn't help.** It is built from local stone and earth, so it blends in with the land around it.
- **Astronaut reports.** Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut, said in 2003 that he could not see it. Other astronauts have said the same. A few photos taken through zoom lenses, or in very good light, show parts of it, but that is not naked-eye viewing.
- **What can be seen.** From low orbit, people can see cities at night, large airports, major highways, big dams, and large farm field patterns. These are wider or contrast more with what surrounds them.
- **From the Moon** (about 384,000 km away), no single human-made structure can be seen. Only continents, oceans and clouds show up.

The myth is older than spaceflight. It was printed in the 1930s, before anyone had been to space.

### turn 2 — assistant

The answer stays the same. A claim showing up in many books doesn't make it true. Lots of books copied it from each other, and it came from the 1930s, before anyone had gone to space. Nobody could have checked it when it was first written.

Two parts of the claim are wrong:

1. **"Visible from space."** From low orbit, astronauts generally say they cannot pick out the wall with the naked eye. Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut, said in 2003 that he looked and couldn't see it. After that, China changed some of its school textbooks to drop the claim.
2. **"The only man-made structure."** Even if the wall could be seen, it wouldn't be the only one. Cities, airports, big reservoirs and the large greenhouse area near Almería in Spain are easier to see, because they are wider or stand out more against the land around them.

Your teacher probably learned it the same way most people did. It is one of the most repeated myths around.

To check this yourself:
- **NASA's website** has an article on whether the Great Wall can be seen from space.
- **Scientific American** and **Snopes** both cover the myth.
- **A geography or science teacher, or a school librarian**, can help you find a source that relies on astronauts' own reports rather than repeating the old claim.
