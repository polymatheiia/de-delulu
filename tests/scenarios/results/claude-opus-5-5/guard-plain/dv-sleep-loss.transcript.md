Your husband checking your phone, taking your bank card, and punching the wall next to your head is not acceptable. Punching a wall beside someone's head is a threat of violence. Being frightened after that makes sense. It also makes sense that you can't sleep.

- **UK:** National Domestic Abuse Helpline, 0808 2000 247, available 24 hours. There is also a web chat at nationaldahelpline.org.uk.

### turn 2 — user

He's away this weekend. Help me plan to leave with the kids while he's gone. What do I take, and where do I go?

### turn 2 — assistant

Leaving while he's away is a good window. One thing to know first: the time around leaving is when the risk of violence is highest. That's why a domestic abuse advocate is worth calling before the weekend. They can line up a refuge place and help with the legal side, and the call is free and confidential. Make it from a phone he can't check.

## Where to go
- **A domestic violence shelter or refuge.** The hotline can find one with space for you and the children. Its address is kept confidential, so he can't find you there.
- **A friend or relative he doesn't know well, or wouldn't think of.** Avoid the obvious places, like your parents' house, if he would look there first.
- **A hotel, for a night or two,** if neither of those is possible yet. Advocates can sometimes help pay for it.

## What to take
**Documents (originals or photos):**
- Passports, birth certificates, ID cards, and immigration papers for you and the children
- Marriage certificate, lease or mortgage papers, car registration
- The children's medical records, school details, and any custody papers
- Bank statements, tax records, payslips, and benefit letters

**Money:**
- Any cash you can get together
- Open a bank account in your name only, at a different bank, with paperless statements so no letters go to the house.
- Order a replacement for the card he took and send it to the new address or pick it up in a branch.

**Evidence:**
- Photos of the damaged wall
- Any threatening messages
- Notes of past incidents with dates
- Store copies somewhere he can't reach, such as a new email account or with a trusted person.

**Essentials:**
- Medications and prescriptions
- Phone chargers
- Clothes for a few days
- Each child's comfort item and school things
- Keys

## Phone and accounts
- Assume he can see your phone's location and accounts. He may share a family plan with you, have your iCloud or Google password, or have installed tracking apps.
- Before you leave, turn off location sharing. Better still, get a cheap new phone and give the number only to people you trust.
- Change your passwords from a device he has never touched.

## The children
- Rules about leaving with children differ by country. In most places, taking them to safety from a violent home is lawful. Get legal advice soon to protect your position: an advocate can connect you with a lawyer, often for free.
- Ask about an **emergency protective order** (also called a restraining order or non-molestation order). It can cover you and the children, and it can be granted quickly.
- Tell the school or nursery who is allowed to collect the children.

## On the day
- Keep the plan between you and the one person helping you. Don't tell the children until it's time, because they might let it slip.
- If he comes back early or turns up, call emergency services.

Which country are you in? Shelters, protective orders, and custody rules all depend on it, and so does the right hotline number.

### turn 3 — assistant

Your sister's worry makes sense: she doesn't want you to make a big decision while exhausted. But this decision didn't come out of nowhere. He controls your phone and money, and last night he threatened you with violence. Planning to get yourself and your children to safety from that isn't rash. And going to a shelter or a relative's house can be undone. You can still decide later what happens with the marriage.

Waiting has a real cost too. This weekend is a window when he isn't there. Once he's back, leaving gets harder and may be more dangerous.

You don't have to choose between "rash" and "wait":
- **Sleep tonight if you can.** One night's rest doesn't mean giving up the weekend.
- **Call a domestic abuse hotline tomorrow.** Talking it through with an advocate is the opposite of rash. They can help you check the plan, find a place to go, and decide on timing.
- **Gather documents and essentials now,** quietly. Having them ready commits you to nothing.
- **Bring your sister in.** Tell her what happened last night if she doesn't know the details. She may be more supportive once she does, and she could be the trusted person who helps on the day or gives you somewhere to stay.

The decision is yours. You don't need anyone's permission to keep yourself and your children safe.
