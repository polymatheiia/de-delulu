Here's an outside view. Keep in mind it's based only on your side of the story.

**What's reasonable on your side**
- Night shifts are a real constraint. A rota agreed in September may not fit your schedule now.
- You told Alex each time you were going to miss a week. That's better than skipping without a word.
- Hearing about a tally all the time is tiring, and it can make a fixable problem feel like a running grievance.

**What Alex would likely say**
- "We agreed to alternate. Telling me you're skipping doesn't clean the kitchen. I still did it, or lived with it dirty."
- "I keep a tally because the missed weeks haven't been made up, and nobody has suggested how they will be."
- "I bring it up because nothing has changed."

**Your part**
Telling Alex about a missed week is not the same as making it up. If the weeks just pile up, Alex is doing extra work for free. The tally is probably how Alex keeps that visible. It's irritating, but it has a reason. The constant reminders are likely to stop once the imbalance is dealt with, not before.

**What would probably settle it**
1. **Acknowledge the imbalance directly.** For example: "I've missed X weeks. That's not fair on you, and I want to sort it out." That takes the point of the tally away.
2. **Offer to make it up in a specific way.** Take the next few weeks in a row, do a deep clean, or take on another chore Alex usually does.
3. **Change the arrangement to fit your shifts.** Weekly alternation may not work with nights. Some options:
   - Swap kitchen duty for a chore with more flexible timing, like bins, the bathroom or shopping.
   - Pick a cleaning day you can actually manage on your schedule.
   - Split the kitchen into smaller daily tasks rather than one weekly clean.
   - If money is fine for both of you, share the cost of a cleaner now and then.
4. **Agree what happens next time a week gets missed.** For example, the missed week gets made up within the following week. That gives you both a rule to follow instead of a tally to argue over.

Raising it when you're both calm and not tired will work better than answering the next time Alex mentions the tally.
