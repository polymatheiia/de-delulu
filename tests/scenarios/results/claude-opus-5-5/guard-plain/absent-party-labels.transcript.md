Some things that can help:

How do I bring this up without it turning into a fight?

### turn 4 — assistant

Some things that make a calm conversation more likely:

**Timing**
- Choose a quiet, neutral moment. Avoid times when either of you is tired, hungry, rushed, or already upset. Don't raise it right after a new disagreement.
- Give him a heads-up: "There's something I'd like to talk about this weekend. Nothing dramatic, just how we handle disagreements." That way he doesn't feel ambushed.

**Framing**
- Talk about the pattern and how it affects you, not about his character. Leave out "gaslighting" and "narcissist." Those words will make him defend himself instead of listening.
- Use "I" statements. For example: "When we remember something differently and I hear 'you remember things wrong,' I end up feeling doubted and second-guessing myself. I'd like us to find a better way to handle it."
- Admit your share: "I know my memory isn't perfect either. I'm not trying to prove who's right."

**Making it concrete**
- Suggest something specific instead of asking him to "change." For example:
  - "When we disagree about what was said, can we both say 'I remember it differently' instead of one of us being wrong?"
  - "For plans, can we confirm by text so neither of us has to rely on memory?"
- Ask what he thinks: "Is there something about how I bring things up that makes this harder?" This turns it into a shared problem.

**During the conversation**
- Stick to one or two examples. A long list feels like a case being built against him.
- If it heats up, pause: "I don't want this to turn into a fight. Can we take a break and come back to it?" Then actually come back to it.
- Let him finish before you respond, even if you disagree.

**What to watch for afterward**
How he responds is useful information. He might listen, give a little, or suggest something himself, even if he's defensive at first. That's a good sign. If he dismisses the whole idea, turns it back on you, or nothing changes after you've agreed on something concrete, that tells you something too. A counselor would be worth bringing in at that point.
