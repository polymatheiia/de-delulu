### turn 1 — user

What's the current population estimate of Poland, and where does the figure come from?

### turn 1 — assistant

The latest official estimate from Statistics Poland (GUS, Główny Urząd Statystyczny), Poland's national statistics office, is about **37.24 million** residents at the end of June 2026. That is down about 159,000 from a year earlier.

**Where the figure comes from:**
- GUS publishes it in its monthly bulletin on the country's social and economic situation ("Sytuacja społeczno-gospodarcza kraju").
- It is a **preliminary** figure. GUS takes the last census (2021) and updates it each period with registered births, deaths and migration.
- Earlier points in the same series: 37.33 million at the end of 2025 and 37.28 million at the end of Q1 2026.
- A later GUS release appears to put the figure near **37.2 million** for September 2026. The page's dates were unclear, so treat that number as provisional.

**A second, higher figure:** GUS has also published experimental estimates built from administrative records. These count more foreign residents and put the end-2025 population at about **38.8 million**. Some news reports about the population "edging up" use this measure. The method differs from the official series. For standard comparisons, use the ~37.2–37.3 million figure.

**Projections:** Under GUS's low-fertility scenario, the population falls to about 29.4 million by 2060. Eurostat projects a drop of about 31.6% by 2100.

### turn 2 — user

And what was it in 2000? Give me the source for that one too.

### turn 2 — assistant

Poland had about **38.25 million** people in 2000. The searches did not find a GUS document that shows the exact 2000 row, so the sourcing is weaker than for the 2026 figure.

**What the searches confirmed:**
- **UN World Population Prospects 2024**, as reported by Worldometer, gives **38,258,071** for 2000 (a mid-year estimate).
- **worlddata.info** gives **38.26 million** for 2000, but it does not say where its number comes from.

**What is not yet confirmed:**
- GUS's own figure for 31 December 2000 is roughly **38.254 million**. This number is from memory, not from a GUS page found in these searches.
- GUS revised its 1990s and 2000–2001 numbers down by several hundred thousand after the 2002 census. Older publications may show a higher pre-revision figure of about 38.6 million, which may explain the 38.56 million that appears on some Wikipedia-derived tables. This is also from memory and not confirmed in these results.

To confirm the GUS number, use the GUS report *"Podstawowe informacje o rozwoju demograficznym Polski 2000–2009"* (linked below) or the Rocznik Demograficzny (Demographic Yearbook). Both contain the year-end population balance tables.

**Comparison:** If the 2000 figure is about 38.25 million, Poland has about 1 million fewer people now (37.24 million in mid-2026). Both are GUS-method numbers, so the comparison holds.
