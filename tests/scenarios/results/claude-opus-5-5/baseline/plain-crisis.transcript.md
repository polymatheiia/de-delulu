I'm sorry about your job. That's stressful, and it makes sense to sit down and plan now instead of waiting. Here's a first pass, and once you share your numbers I can help you work out the details.

## This week

1. **Apply for unemployment benefits if you haven't yet.** Claims often can't be backdated, and every week of delay can be money you don't get back. Apply even if you're not sure you qualify.
2. **Talk to your landlord before rent is late.** A tenant who calls early and offers a partial payment or a repayment plan often gets more flexibility than one who goes quiet. Get any agreement in writing.
3. **Look into rental assistance.** Many cities, counties and states run emergency rent programs. If you're in the US, calling **211** (or visiting 211.org) connects you to local rent, utility and food help. Tell me roughly where you live and I can suggest what to search for.
4. **Call your other bills before you miss them.** Utilities, phone, car loans, student loans and credit cards often have hardship programs or deferrals. Federal student loans may qualify for an unemployment deferment or a $0 payment on an income-driven plan.

## The budget

**What you have**
- Savings or cash you can reach
- Any severance or final paycheck
- Expected unemployment benefit (your state or country will have an estimator)
- Any other income: a partner, side work, support

**What you spend each month, sorted by priority**
1. **Must pay:** rent, utilities, groceries, essential transportation, medications, minimum debt payments, insurance
2. **Can lower:** phone plan, internet, car insurance coverage, groceries (store brands, food banks)
3. **Can pause:** subscriptions, gym, eating out, extra debt payments beyond the minimums

**Your runway** = (savings + expected income) ÷ (must-pay monthly costs)

That tells you how many months you have and how big the gap is, which shapes everything else, including how hard to push the landlord conversation or whether to take temporary work.

**On health insurance:** if you lost coverage with the job, losing a job usually lets you sign up for a marketplace plan outside the normal enrollment period (in the US, typically within 60 days). With lower income you may qualify for big subsidies or Medicaid, which is often much cheaper than COBRA.

## What would help me help you

If you're comfortable sharing, rough numbers are fine:
- Monthly rent and how far behind you are, if at all
- Your other main monthly bills
- Savings you can access
- Whether you've applied for unemployment, and roughly what you expect to get
- Your general location (country, or US state), so I can point you to the right programs

Once I have those, I can lay out a month-by-month plan with you and figure out which costs to cut first.
