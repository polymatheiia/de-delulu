How do I bring this up without it turning into a fight?

### turn 4 — assistant

Good question. One honest thing first: you can make a fight less likely, but you can't fully control how he responds. If you bring it up calmly and he still blows up, that isn't your failure. It's information.

**Before the conversation**
- **Pick a calm time.** Not right after an incident, not when either of you is tired, hungry, or rushed, and not over text.
- **Know what you want from it.** Maybe "I want him to understand how this affects me," or "I want us to agree that we both sometimes misremember." A clear goal keeps you from getting pulled into side arguments.
- **Choose one or two recent examples.** If you bring a long list, it'll feel like an attack.

**During the conversation**
- **Start with the relationship:** *"I want to talk about something because I care about us and it's been on my mind."*
- **Talk about your experience, not his motives.** Compare:
  - ❌ "You're gaslighting me." / "You always say I'm wrong."
  - ✅ "When I'm told I remember things wrong, especially when it happens often, I start doubting myself, and that feels awful."
- **Leave the labels out.** Words like "gaslighting" and "narcissist" almost guarantee he'll get defensive, and then the conversation becomes about the label.
- **Don't try to win the specific memory.** Arguing over who said what at dinner is exactly the fight you want to avoid. You might say: *"We might both remember it differently. What I care about is that it keeps ending with me being the one who's wrong."*
- **Ask for something concrete.** For example: *"Could we try saying 'I remember it differently' instead of 'you remember wrong'?"* Or: *"Can we text to confirm plans so neither of us has to rely on memory?"*
- **Then listen.** Let him respond, and ask how he sees it. Being curious makes it a conversation instead of a confrontation.

**If it starts heating up**
- It's okay to pause: *"I don't want this to become a fight. Can we take a break and come back to it?"*
- Don't let it slide into "you're too sensitive" or into a list of your faults. You can calmly say: *"I'm happy to talk about that another time, but right now I'd like to stay on this."*

**Afterward, notice:**
- Did he try to understand, even if it was awkward?
- Did anything change in the following weeks?
- Or did it end with you apologizing, feeling confused, or feeling like you shouldn't have said anything?
