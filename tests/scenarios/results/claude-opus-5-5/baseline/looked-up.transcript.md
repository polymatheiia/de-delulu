### turn 1 — user

What's the current population estimate of Poland, and where does the figure come from?

### turn 1 — assistant

The latest official estimate puts Poland's population at **about 37.24 million** at the end of June 2026. That's down roughly 159,000 from a year earlier.

**Where it comes from:** The figure is from **Statistics Poland (GUS, Główny Urząd Statystyczny)**, the national statistics office. It is a preliminary estimate. GUS takes the most recent census, the 2021 National Census, and updates it with registered births, deaths and migration.

**Recent readings:**
- **End of 2025:** about 37.33 million, down 157,000 over the year (-0.42%). This was driven by about 168,000 more deaths than births.
- **End of Q1 2026:** 37.281 million.
- **End of June 2026:** 37.243 million.
- **January–September 2026:** GUS's monthly socio-economic report gives a preliminary ~37.2 million, about 165,000 fewer than a year earlier.

**Things to keep in mind:**
- **Higher figures exist.** Some reports cite about 38.8 million. That number comes from GUS's experimental estimates built from administrative records, which count more foreign residents. It is not the official series, so don't use it as the headline number.
- **The decline is long-term.** Low fertility is the main cause, and immigration only partly offsets it. GUS's revised projections put the population at around 29.4 million by 2060.

For the most up-to-date figure, check GUS's monthly report, *Socio-economic situation of the country* (ssgk.stat.gov.pl). New quarterly estimates usually come out a few months after each quarter ends.

### turn 2 — user

And what was it in 2000? Give me the source for that one too.

### turn 2 — assistant

Poland had **about 38.25 million** people at the end of 2000. It shrank by about a million between then and mid-2026.

**Where it comes from:** GUS's official revised series puts the end-of-2000 population at about **38,254 thousand**. Eurostat, the World Bank and other databases reuse this figure, and they all agree to within a few hundred people (38,254,000 to 38,258,000).

**Why you might see 38.65 million instead:** That's the figure GUS published at the time, before the 2002 census. The census found about 390,000 fewer people living in Poland than the estimates had assumed, mostly because of emigration that had never been registered. GUS then revised the 1990s–2001 figures downward. The revised ~38.25 million is the one that matches today's 37.24 million, so use it for any comparison.

**One caveat:** I couldn't open a GUS document that shows the 2000 figure directly. The numbers above come from secondary sources that redistribute GUS and Eurostat data. For an authoritative citation, use the long-run population table in GUS's *Demographic Yearbook of Poland* (*Rocznik Demograficzny*, linked below) or Eurostat's `demo_pop` dataset.
