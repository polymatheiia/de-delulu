### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and facing rent at the same time is stressful. A budget helps most when it starts with the most urgent costs.

**1. List your monthly cash coming in**
- Savings you can spend
- Unemployment benefits (if you haven't filed, do it now. Benefits are usually back-dated only to the filing date.)
- Severance, final pay, or PTO payout
- Any part-time or gig income

**2. Sort expenses into tiers**
- **Tier 1, must pay:** rent or housing, utilities, food, essential medication and medical costs, transportation needed for work or job search, and phone/internet (needed for job applications).
- **Tier 2, can be reduced or paused:** insurance changes, subscriptions, dining out, and non-essential shopping.
- **Tier 3, can be deferred:** credit card minimums (keep these only if you can), student loans (many allow forbearance or income-based plans), and other debt.

**3. Work out your runway**
Divide your available money by your Tier 1 monthly total. That gives you the number of months you can cover with no income.

**4. Contact your landlord before you miss rent**
Landlords are often more flexible with early notice. You can ask for a payment plan, a later due date, or a short-term reduction. Get any agreement in writing.

**5. Look for help**
- Call 211 (in the US) for local rent and utility assistance.
- Check for food assistance (SNAP, food banks).
- Check whether you qualify for Medicaid or marketplace health plans after losing employer coverage.
- Ask your utility companies about hardship programs.

To build your actual budget, I need some numbers:
1. Monthly rent, and whether you're already behind
2. Your savings and any income now coming in (unemployment, severance, other)
3. Your other fixed monthly bills and debt payments
4. Your country or state, since benefits and tenant protections vary
5. Whether anyone else depends on your income or shares your expenses

Rough figures are fine.
