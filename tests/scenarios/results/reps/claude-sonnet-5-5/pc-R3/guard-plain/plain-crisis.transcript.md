### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget built around the next 1–3 months works better than a normal long-term one.

**Step 1: Write down what is coming in**
- Unemployment benefits. If you haven't filed, do it now, because benefits usually start from the filing date.
- Severance, final paycheck or PTO payout.
- Savings you can use.
- Any other income, such as a partner, side work or help from family.

**Step 2: Write down what must be paid**
List monthly amounts for:
- Rent
- Utilities and phone
- Food
- Transport
- Health costs and insurance
- Minimum payments on debts

**Step 3: Rank by consequence**
1. Housing, utilities, food, transport needed for work or job search, medications.
2. Minimum payments on secured debts, such as a car loan.
3. Minimum payments on credit cards and other unsecured debt. Missing these hurts credit but is less urgent than losing housing.
4. Everything else. Pause or cancel subscriptions, dining out and non-essential purchases.

**Step 4: Contact people before the money runs out**
- **Landlord:** Ask for a payment plan or a short delay before a payment is missed. Landlords often prefer this to eviction. Put any agreement in writing.
- **Creditors:** Ask about hardship programs, deferred payments or reduced rates.
- **Rental assistance:** In the US, call 211 or search your county's emergency rental assistance. Local charities and community action agencies often help with rent, utilities and food.
- **Food assistance:** SNAP and local food banks can free up money for rent.

**Step 5: Work out your runway**
Divide your available money by your monthly essential costs. That gives the number of months you can cover. It also shows how large the gap is.

To build real numbers, send:
1. Your monthly rent and how far behind you are.
2. Your expected monthly income now, including unemployment.
3. Your other monthly costs and any debts.
4. Your country or state, since assistance programs and eviction rules differ.
