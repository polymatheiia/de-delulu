### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget built around the next 1-3 months works best here.

**1. Find your real runway**
- Add up cash on hand and anything that can be accessed quickly.
- Add expected income: severance, unemployment benefits, partner or side income.
- Divide by monthly essentials to see how many months you have.

**2. Sort expenses into tiers**
- **Tier 1, protect first:** rent/housing, utilities, food, essential medication and health costs, transport needed for work or job searching, and phone/internet.
- **Tier 2, pay minimums only:** credit cards, loans, and other debts.
- **Tier 3, pause or cut:** subscriptions, dining out, non-essential shopping, and extras.

**3. Claim income support now**
- File for unemployment benefits right away if you haven't. Delays often mean lost weeks of payment.
- Check eligibility for food assistance (SNAP in the US), utility assistance, and health coverage.

**4. Talk to your landlord before rent is missed**
- Ask for a payment plan, a delayed due date, or a partial payment arrangement. Get any agreement in writing.
- Look for local emergency rental assistance. In the US, calling 211 or searching your county's housing assistance program is a good start.
- Learn your local eviction process and timelines. Many areas have free tenant legal aid.

**5. Contact lenders and creditors**
- Many offer hardship programs, deferrals, or lower payments, but only if you ask.
- Prioritize secured debts and essentials over unsecured ones like credit cards.

**6. Add income, even temporary**
- Gig work, temp agencies, or part-time work can cover gaps while you search.

To build actual numbers, send these figures:
1. Monthly rent and other fixed bills
2. Cash and savings available
3. Any current or expected income, including unemployment benefits
4. Debts and their minimum payments
5. Your country or state, since assistance programs differ

With those, you can get a month-by-month plan and a clear view of how much rent shortfall you're facing.
