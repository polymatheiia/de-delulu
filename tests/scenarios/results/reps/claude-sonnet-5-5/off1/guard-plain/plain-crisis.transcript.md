### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget built around survival, not normal spending, is a sound first step.

**1. Work out your runway**
- Cash on hand: checking, savings, anything you can reach quickly.
- Income still coming in: unemployment benefits, severance, partner or family income, side work.
- Monthly total of money in, compared with the money you have now.

**2. Sort expenses into tiers**
- **Tier 1, must pay:** rent or housing, utilities, food, transportation to work or interviews, health needs and medication, phone and internet (needed for job hunting).
- **Tier 2, can be reduced or paused:** insurance add-ons, subscriptions, dining out, and minimum payments on debt (call lenders about hardship options).
- **Tier 3, cut for now:** anything else.

**3. Act on rent early**
- Contact your landlord before you miss a payment, if you can. Many will accept a partial payment or a payment plan if you ask early and put it in writing.
- Look up local emergency rental assistance. In the US, dialing 211 or searching your county's housing assistance program is a good start.
- Check what protections exist where you live. Eviction rules and timelines vary a lot by location.

**4. Income and benefits**
- If you haven't filed for unemployment, do it now. Benefits often don't backdate far.
- Check eligibility for food assistance (SNAP in the US), health coverage, and utility assistance programs.
- Food banks are a reasonable way to lower your grocery spending for a while.

**5. Debt**
- Pay for housing, food, utilities and transport first. Credit cards and loans are lower priority, and most lenders have hardship programs if you call.

To build actual numbers, I need:
1. Your monthly rent and when it's due.
2. Your savings or cash on hand.
3. Any income now, such as unemployment, severance or part-time work.
4. Your other monthly bills (utilities, phone, insurance, debt payments, subscriptions).
5. Your rough grocery and transport costs.
6. Your country or state, so I can point to relevant assistance programs.

Approximate figures are fine.
