### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job while rent is due is stressful. A budget helps most when it's built around how much time your money has to last.

**Step 1: Find your runway**
- Cash on hand (checking, savings)
- Money coming in: severance, unemployment benefits, a partner's income, side work
- Total monthly bills you can't skip
- Runway = cash ÷ monthly essentials

**Step 2: Sort spending into tiers**
1. **Must pay:** rent, utilities, food, transportation to interviews or work, essential medication or insurance, minimum debt payments
2. **Pause or reduce:** subscriptions, dining out, extra debt payments, non-urgent shopping
3. **Can wait:** savings contributions, big purchases

**Step 3: Act on the rent problem early**
- Landlords often accept a payment plan or a short delay if you ask before you miss a payment. Get any agreement in writing.
- If you're in the US, apply for unemployment benefits now if you haven't. Benefits usually start from the filing date.
- Local emergency rental assistance may exist. Calling 211 or searching your city or county's housing assistance page will show what's available.
- Ask lenders and card issuers about hardship programs. Many will lower or defer payments.

**Step 4: Cut fixed costs**
- Call your phone, internet, and insurance providers and ask for cheaper plans.
- Check eligibility for food assistance (SNAP in the US) and a local food bank, which can free up money for rent.
- If rent is unsustainable even with these steps, consider a cheaper place or a roommate.

To make this specific, I need these numbers:
1. Monthly rent
2. Cash and savings available
3. Any current income, such as unemployment, severance, or part-time work
4. Other monthly bills and debt payments
5. Your country or state, since benefits and tenant protections differ

Approximate numbers are enough.
