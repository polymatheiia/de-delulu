### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget built around a lower or uncertain income can make it more manageable.

**1. Get the numbers down**
- **Money in:** unemployment benefits (if you've applied), severance, savings, and any side income or help from others.
- **Money out:** rent, utilities, phone, food, insurance, transport, debt minimums, subscriptions.
- **Cash on hand:** total in checking and savings.

**2. Sort expenses into tiers**
- **Tier 1, must pay:** housing, utilities, food, essential transport, medication and health costs.
- **Tier 2, pay if possible:** minimum payments on debt, phone and internet.
- **Tier 3, pause or cut:** subscriptions, dining out, non-essential shopping.

**3. Work out your runway**
Divide your cash by your monthly Tier 1 costs. That shows how many months you can cover. It also shows how much income you need to close the gap.

**4. Act on rent now**
- **Tell your landlord early.** Landlords often prefer a payment plan to an eviction. Put any agreement in writing.
- **Look for rental assistance.** Try 211 (in the US), local housing authorities, and community action agencies. Many have emergency funds for people who recently lost income.
- **Learn your local eviction process.** Notice periods and protections vary. Local legal aid or tenant rights groups can explain yours, often for free.

**5. Increase income and reduce costs**
- File for unemployment right away if you haven't. Benefits can be backdated only so far.
- Ask utilities and lenders about hardship programs.
- Check eligibility for food assistance and, if you lost employer coverage, health coverage.
- Hold off on withdrawing from retirement accounts. Penalties and taxes make that costly.

To build the actual budget, I need a few figures:
1. Monthly rent and how far behind you are
2. Your current income sources, if any, and the amount of each
3. Cash and savings on hand
4. Your other regular monthly bills
5. Your country or state, so I can point to relevant programs

Rough numbers are fine.
