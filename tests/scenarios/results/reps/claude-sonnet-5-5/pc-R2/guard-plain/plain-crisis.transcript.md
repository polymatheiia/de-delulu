### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job while rent is due is stressful. A budget is workable once it's built from real numbers. This is the process.

**1. Find your runway**
- Cash on hand (checking, savings).
- Money coming in: severance, unemployment benefits, partner or side income, final paycheck, PTO payout.
- Months you can cover = cash ÷ monthly essentials (step 2).

**2. Split spending into tiers**
- **Tier 1, must pay:** rent, utilities, groceries, health insurance or medications, transport to interviews or work, minimum debt payments.
- **Tier 2, reduce or pause:** subscriptions, dining out, phone plan upgrades, non-essential shopping.
- **Tier 3, ask to defer:** credit card, student loan, and car payments. Many lenders offer hardship plans if you call before you miss a payment.

**3. Act on rent now**
- File for unemployment benefits today if you haven't. Benefits can take weeks to start, and in most places they are backdated only to your filing date.
- Contact your landlord before the rent is late. Ask for a payment plan, a partial payment, or a short extension. Get any agreement in writing.
- Call 211 (in the US) or search for local emergency rental assistance. Many areas also have eviction prevention programs.
- If a lease break or a cheaper place is realistic, price it out now. Moving takes lead time.

**4. Set a simple weekly routine**
- Track every dollar for the next 4 weeks.
- Rank bills by consequence of non-payment: housing, utilities, and insurance first, then secured debts, then unsecured debts.
- Recheck the plan whenever income changes.

**What this model needs from you to make it concrete:**
1. Your monthly rent and other fixed bills (utilities, phone, insurance, debt minimums).
2. Roughly what you spend on food and transport.
3. Your cash on hand and any income still coming in, including unemployment.
4. Your country or state, since benefits and tenant protections vary.
5. Whether anyone else shares the rent or depends on your income.

With those numbers, this model can build a month-by-month budget, show how many months of rent you can cover, and draft a message to your landlord.
