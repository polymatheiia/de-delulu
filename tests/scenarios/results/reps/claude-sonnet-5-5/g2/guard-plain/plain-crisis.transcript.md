### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job while rent is due is stressful. A budget can show how long your money lasts and which costs to cut or delay first.

**1. Find your runway**
- Add up cash and savings you can access.
- Add expected income: unemployment benefits, severance, final pay, PTO payout, any part-time or gig work.
- Divide total money by monthly essential costs. The result is how many months you can cover.

**2. List essentials only**
Cover these first: rent or housing, utilities, food, transportation, health insurance or medications, and minimum debt payments. Everything else is secondary for now.

**3. Pause or cut the rest**
- Cancel or pause subscriptions.
- Cut dining out and non-essential shopping.
- Call providers like phone, internet, and insurance and ask for cheaper plans.

**4. Deal with rent early**
- Contact your landlord before you miss a payment. Landlords often prefer a payment plan or a short extension to an eviction.
- Get any agreement in writing.
- Look up local emergency rental assistance. In the US, calling 211 or searching your county's name plus "emergency rental assistance" is a good start.

**5. Claim every benefit available**
- File for unemployment right away if you haven't. Benefits are often counted from the filing date.
- Check eligibility for SNAP, Medicaid, and utility assistance. Food banks can lower your grocery spending.

**6. Order your bills by priority**
Pay in this order: housing, utilities, food, transportation, and insurance. Credit cards, student loans, and other unsecured debts come after. Many lenders offer hardship programs if you ask.

To make this specific, I need a few numbers:
- Monthly rent and other essential costs
- Savings and cash on hand
- Any income coming in, such as unemployment, severance, or side work
- Debts and their minimum payments
- Your country or state, since assistance programs differ

Rough estimates are fine.
