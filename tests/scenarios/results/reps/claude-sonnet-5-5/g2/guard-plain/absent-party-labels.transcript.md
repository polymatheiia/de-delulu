How do I bring this up without it turning into a fight?

### turn 4 — assistant

There's no way to guarantee it won't turn into a fight, but some choices make it less likely.

**Timing**
- Pick a calm moment. Don't raise it right after a missed plan or during an argument.
- Ask first: "Can we talk about something that's been bothering me? Is now okay, or would later tonight be better?" That gives him a chance to be ready.

**Opening**
- Start with the goal, not the accusation: "I want us to feel like we can count on each other's plans, and I think we've had some mix-ups."
- Use "I" statements about your experience: "I felt let down when dinner didn't happen," not "You always forget."

**Content**
- Pick one or two concrete examples. Don't list everything.
- Don't reopen the question of who agreed to the second dinner. Say: "I know we remember that one differently. What I'd like is a way to avoid that."
- Describe the "you remember it wrong" moments by their effect: "When I mention something you said and the answer is that I remember wrong, I feel shut down. I'd like us to check together instead."

**Ask for something specific**
- Confirm plans by text.
- If one of you remembers something differently, say "I remember it differently" and look for a record. Don't say "you're wrong."

**Listen**
- Ask: "How does this look from your side?" Then let him answer without interrupting. He may say something that changes how you see it.

**If it heats up**
- Agree beforehand that either of you can pause and come back later: "I want to keep talking about this, but I'm getting upset. Can we take twenty minutes?"
- Don't use the pause to avoid the topic. Name a time to return.

**What to watch**
- If he engages, even awkwardly, that's a good sign.
- If he dismisses it, changes the subject, or turns it back on you each time you raise it calmly, that is useful to know. Then a couples counselor or a trusted friend could help.
