### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget built around a lower income is a workable first step.

**1. Find your runway**
- Add up cash and savings you can use.
- Add expected income: unemployment benefits, severance, part-time or gig work, and help from others.
- Divide the cash by your monthly essential costs. That gives you the number of months you can cover.

**2. Sort expenses into tiers**
- **Tier 1, keep paying:** rent or housing, utilities, food, essential medication and health costs, transport to work or interviews, and phone or internet for job searching.
- **Tier 2, reduce or pause:** subscriptions, dining out, non-essential shopping, and extra streaming or gym fees.
- **Tier 3, minimum only or negotiate:** credit cards, loans, and other debt. Pay minimums, or ask for hardship options.

**3. Act on rent now**
- Contact your landlord before you miss a payment. Many will agree to a payment plan or a partial payment, especially if you ask early and in writing.
- Look for local emergency rental assistance. In the US, 211.org and your county's housing office list programs.
- Check your lease and local law on late fees and eviction notice periods. Tenant rights organizations often give free advice.

**4. Cut costs and add income**
- File for unemployment benefits right away if you haven't. Back pay usually starts from the filing date.
- Check eligibility for food assistance, utility assistance, and health coverage.
- Call lenders and utilities and ask about hardship programs. Many offer lower payments or deferrals.
- Consider temporary income such as gig work or part-time shifts while you search.

**What I need to build your numbers**
1. Your monthly rent and your other fixed bills (utilities, phone, insurance, debt minimums).
2. Your rough monthly spending on food, transport, and anything else.
3. Your current savings and any income now (benefits, severance, partial work).
4. Your country or state, since benefits and tenant protections differ.
5. How many months behind you are, if any.

With those, I can lay out a month-by-month plan and show how long your money lasts under a few scenarios.
