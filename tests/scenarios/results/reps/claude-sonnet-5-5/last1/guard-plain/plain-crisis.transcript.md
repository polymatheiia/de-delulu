### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and facing rent is stressful. A budget built around a shorter runway can make it more manageable.

**Numbers to gather**
1. Cash on hand (checking, savings).
2. Income still coming in: unemployment benefits, severance, partner or side income, other.
3. Fixed monthly costs: rent, utilities, phone, insurance, minimum debt payments, transport.
4. Variable costs: groceries, gas, subscriptions, other.
5. Debts and their due dates.

**How to structure it**
1. **Runway:** cash on hand divided by monthly essential costs gives the number of months you can cover.
2. **Tier your spending:**
   - Tier 1: housing, utilities, food, health care, transport to work or interviews.
   - Tier 2: minimum debt payments, phone and internet.
   - Tier 3: everything else (subscriptions, dining out, non-essentials). Cut or pause these first.
3. **Pay in this order:** housing, food, utilities, then debt minimums. Credit cards and loans can usually wait or be renegotiated. Missing rent has the most severe consequences.

**Actions that often help right away**
- **File for unemployment benefits** now if you haven't. Benefits are often counted from the filing date.
- **Talk to your landlord early.** A payment plan or a short delay is easier to get before you miss a payment than after.
- **Call lenders and creditors.** Many offer hardship programs, deferrals, or reduced payments if you ask.
- **Look for local aid:** call 211 (in the US) for emergency rent assistance, food banks, and utility help. Other countries have similar services.
- **Check health coverage options** if your insurance ended with the job.
- **Pause or cancel** subscriptions, and review insurance and phone plans for cheaper options.

If you share your rough numbers and your country or state, I can build a month-by-month budget with you and work out how long your money lasts.
