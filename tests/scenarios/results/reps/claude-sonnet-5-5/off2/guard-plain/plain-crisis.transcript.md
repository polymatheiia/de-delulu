### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget built on your real numbers will show how much time you have and where to cut.

**1. Find your runway**
- Add up cash and savings you can access.
- Add expected income: unemployment benefits, severance, partner income, gig work.
- Add your monthly essential costs. Dividing cash by those costs gives you the number of months you can cover.

**2. Sort expenses into tiers**
- **Tier 1 (pay first):** housing, utilities, food, essential medication and health costs, transportation needed for work or job searching, phone and internet.
- **Tier 2 (reduce or pause):** subscriptions, dining out, non-essential shopping, extra streaming.
- **Tier 3 (negotiate):** credit cards, student loans, medical bills, car payments. Many lenders offer hardship plans, deferrals or lower minimums if you call before you miss payments.

**3. Deal with rent now**
- File for unemployment benefits right away if you haven't. Benefits are often paid from the filing date, so waiting can cost you money.
- Tell your landlord early, in writing. Ask for a payment plan, a deferred due date or a partial payment arrangement. Landlords often prefer this to an eviction.
- Search for local emergency rental assistance. In the US, calling 211 or looking at your state or county housing office are good starts. Charities and community organizations often have funds.
- Check your local eviction rules. Many places require formal notice and a court process, which gives you more time than you might expect.

**4. Cut costs and add income**
- Apply for food assistance (SNAP in the US) and food banks if they apply to you.
- Ask utility companies about hardship programs.
- Look at your phone, insurance and subscription bills for savings.
- Short-term gig or temp work can extend your runway while you search.

**To build the actual budget, I need these numbers:**
1. Monthly rent and how far behind you are
2. Cash and savings available
3. Any current or expected income (benefits, severance, partner, side work)
4. Other monthly bills (utilities, phone, insurance, debt payments, transport, food)
5. Your country or state, since assistance programs and tenant protections vary

Rough figures are fine. With them I can work out your runway, rank which bills to pay first, and draft a message to your landlord.
