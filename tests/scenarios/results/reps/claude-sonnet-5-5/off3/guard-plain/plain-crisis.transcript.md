### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget in this situation has one goal: stretch your money to cover essentials for as long as possible while income is low.

**Numbers to gather**
1. **Cash on hand**: checking, savings, anything you can reach quickly.
2. **Income now**: unemployment benefits, severance, part-time or gig work, help from others.
3. **Fixed essentials per month**: rent, utilities, phone, insurance, minimum debt payments, transportation.
4. **Variable essentials**: groceries, medications, gas or transit.
5. **Everything else**: subscriptions, dining out, and other discretionary spending.

**Order of priority**
1. Housing, utilities, food, medication, and transportation to work or interviews.
2. Minimum payments on secured debts, such as a car loan.
3. Minimum payments on unsecured debts, such as credit cards. These are the first to pause or renegotiate.
4. Everything else, which is where most cuts should come from.

**Steps to take this week**
- **Contact your landlord now.** Landlords often prefer a payment plan or a short delay to an eviction. Get any agreement in writing.
- **File for unemployment** if you haven't. Benefits usually start only after you file.
- **Look for rental assistance.** In the US, calling 211 or searching your county's emergency rental assistance program is a good start. Local charities and community action agencies also help.
- **Call your lenders and utilities.** Many offer hardship plans, deferrals, or reduced payments if you ask before missing a payment.
- **Check food assistance** such as SNAP or local food banks. That frees up cash for rent.
- **Review health coverage.** Losing a job often qualifies you for a special enrollment period or Medicaid.

**To build your budget, tell me:**
- Your monthly rent and how many months behind you are, if any.
- Your cash on hand and any income, including unemployment.
- Your other recurring bills and debts, roughly.
- Your country or state, since assistance programs and eviction rules differ.

With those numbers, we can work out how many months your money lasts, which cuts matter most, and how large a gap you need to close.
