### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent at the same time is stressful. Here's a way to work through it.

## Step 1: Get the real numbers

Write down:

- **Cash on hand:** checking, savings, anything you can get to quickly.
- **Income coming in:** unemployment benefits, severance, final paycheck, side work, help from family.
- **Fixed costs:** rent, utilities, phone, insurance, minimum debt payments, car payment.
- **Variable costs:** groceries, gas or transit, medications, everything else.

Look at the last 1–2 months of bank statements. Going from memory usually comes out too low.

## Step 2: Work out your runway

**Runway (months) = cash on hand ÷ (monthly costs − monthly income)**

This number tells you how urgent things are. Two months and eight months call for different decisions.

## Step 3: Pay things in priority order

When there isn't enough for everything, this order usually protects you best:

1. **Housing:** rent, or at least a partial payment and a plan you've agreed with your landlord.
2. **Utilities:** power, water, heat.
3. **Food and medication.**
4. **Transportation** you need for job hunting or work.
5. **Phone and internet,** kept to the cheapest plan that works for job searching.
6. **Minimum payments on secured debt** like a car loan.
7. **Unsecured debt:** credit cards, personal loans. Missing these hurts your credit, but you won't lose your housing over them. Many lenders offer hardship programs if you call.

Cut or pause anything that isn't on this list, like subscriptions and memberships.

## Step 4: Deal with rent directly

- **Talk to your landlord before the rent is late, or as soon as it is.** A partial payment plus a written plan often goes better than saying nothing. Keep everything in writing.
- **Emergency rental assistance:** in the US, call **211** or go to 211.org. They connect you to local rent help, utility help and food programs. Other countries have similar services.
- **Unemployment benefits:** if you haven't filed, do it now. Benefits usually start from the date you file, not the date you lost the job.
- **Utility hardship programs:** most utility companies have them, and some don't advertise them.
- **Food assistance** (SNAP in the US, food banks) frees up cash for rent.
- **If you get an eviction notice,** contact local legal aid right away. Eviction rules vary a lot by location, and tenants often have more time and rights than the notice suggests.

## Step 5: Bring in some money

Even small, irregular income stretches your runway. Temp agencies, gig work and part-time shifts can fill the gap while you look for a longer-term job.
