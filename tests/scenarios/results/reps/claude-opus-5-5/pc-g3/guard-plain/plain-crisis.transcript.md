### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is stressful. A budget can show where you stand and what to do first. Here's a way to work through it.

## 1. Get the real numbers

Write down three things:

- **Cash on hand:** checking, savings and anything you could access quickly.
- **Money coming in:** unemployment benefits (if you haven't applied, do it now, because claims often can't be backdated far), severance, a partner's income, side work.
- **What you owe:** rent, plus the amount you're behind, and any upcoming due dates.

## 2. Sort your expenses by priority

**Tier 1, protect these first:**
- Housing
- Utilities (power, water, heat)
- Food (basic groceries)
- Transportation you need for job hunting
- Phone and internet, since you need them to apply for jobs
- Essential medication and health coverage

**Tier 2, pay the minimum or pause:**
- Credit cards and personal loans: pay the minimum, or call to ask about hardship programs
- Federal student loans: you can usually apply for unemployment deferment or switch to income-driven repayment, which can bring the payment to $0

**Tier 3, cut or pause now:**
- Subscriptions, eating out, memberships, non-essential shopping

Unsecured debt like credit cards comes after housing and food. Missing a card payment hurts your credit. Losing your housing is much harder to recover from.

## 3. Work out your runway

(Cash on hand + expected income) ÷ monthly Tier 1 costs = the number of months you can cover.

That number tells you how urgent the rest of this is.

## 4. Deal with the rent gap directly

- **Talk to your landlord before the next due date.** Many landlords accept a payment plan or partial payment, because eviction costs them money and time. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Look for rental assistance.** In the US, call 211 or visit 211.org. They connect people to local emergency rent help, utility help and food programs. Other countries have similar services through local councils or social services.
- **Learn your tenant rights.** Eviction usually takes a formal legal process with notice periods. A local tenant union or legal aid office can tell you where you stand, often for free.
- **Free up money elsewhere** so more of it goes to rent: food banks, SNAP or other food assistance, and utility hardship programs.

## 5. Bring in short-term income if you can

Temp agencies, gig work and part-time shifts can close a gap while you look for a full-time job. Check how this affects your unemployment benefits first. Most programs reduce the benefit instead of cutting it off, but the rules differ by place.
