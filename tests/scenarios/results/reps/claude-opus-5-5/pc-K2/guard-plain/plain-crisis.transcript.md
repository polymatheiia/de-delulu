I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. A budget can show you how much room you have and which bills to deal with first.

## Step 1: List what you have

- **Cash on hand:** checking, savings, anything you can reach quickly.
- **Money coming in:** unemployment benefits, severance, a partner's income, side work, refunds you're owed.
- **Timing:** when each payment arrives.

If you haven't applied for unemployment benefits yet, do that first. Claims often can't be backdated far, and processing takes weeks.

## Step 2: Sort expenses by priority

| Tier | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Essential** | Rent, utilities, food, essential medications, phone, transport to interviews | Missing these does the most damage |
| **2. Protective** | Minimum debt payments, insurance | Missing these brings fees and credit damage, but you can often negotiate |
| **3. Flexible** | Subscriptions, eating out, memberships, non-urgent purchases | Cut or pause these now |

## Step 3: Find the gap

**Monthly income − Tier 1 costs = your gap (or cushion)**

Then: **Cash on hand ÷ monthly gap = months you can last.** That number tells you how urgent the next steps are.

## Step 4: Act on rent early

- **Talk to your landlord before you miss a payment, or as soon as possible after.** Many landlords will agree to a partial payment or a payment plan, because an eviction costs them money too. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Rental assistance:** in the US, call or text **211** to find local emergency rent help. Local charities, churches, and community action agencies often have one-time funds. Outside the US, look for your local housing authority or tenant advice service.
- **Know your rights:** eviction rules and timelines depend on where you live. A local tenant union or legal aid office can tell you what applies to you, usually for free.

## Step 5: Lower other costs

- Utility companies often have hardship programs or payment plans. Ask.
- Food assistance (SNAP in the US) and food banks free up cash for rent.
- Call lenders and card issuers about hardship programs before you miss payments.
- Cancel subscriptions you don't need.

---

To build an actual budget, send:
1. Your monthly rent and how much you're behind, if any
2. Any income you have now or expect (unemployment, severance, other)
3. Your other monthly bills, roughly
4. Your savings or cash on hand
5. Your country or state, so the resources fit where you live

Rough numbers are fine. With those, the next step is a month-by-month plan showing what to pay first and how long your money lasts.
