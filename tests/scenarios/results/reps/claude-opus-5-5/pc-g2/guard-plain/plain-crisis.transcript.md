### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent at the same time is stressful. Here's a way to work through it.

## 1. Know how long your money lasts

Write down:
- **Cash on hand:** checking, savings, and any final paycheck or severance still to come.
- **Expected income:** unemployment benefits, side work, or a partner's income.
- **Debts due soon:** rent owed, credit card minimums, loans.

Cash plus expected income, divided by your monthly costs, tells you how many weeks or months you have. That number decides how hard you need to cut.

## 2. Sort your expenses into tiers

| Tier | Examples | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Essentials** | Rent, utilities, groceries, medications, phone, transport to interviews | Protect these first |
| **Negotiable** | Insurance, internet plan, car payment, minimum debt payments | Call providers and ask for hardship plans or lower rates |
| **Pausable** | Subscriptions, eating out, gym, non-urgent shopping | Cancel or pause now |

The pausable tier often adds up to a few hundred a month. The negotiable tier can save more, but it takes phone calls.

## 3. Deal with rent directly

- **Talk to your landlord before the rent is late, or as soon as possible if it already is.** Many landlords accept a payment plan or partial payment because evicting someone costs them money too. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Look for rental assistance.** In the US, dial **211** or visit 211.org for local emergency rent programs. Local charities and religious groups often run them too.
- **Learn your tenant rights.** Eviction rules and notice periods vary by state and country. A local tenant union or legal aid office can explain them for free.

## 4. Bring in money

- **File for unemployment right away** if you haven't already. Back pay is often limited, so delays can cost you.
- **Check other programs:** SNAP (food assistance), Medicaid, and utility assistance (LIHEAP in the US). Using these frees up cash for rent.
- **Short-term income:** temp agencies, gig work, or part-time jobs can cover the gap while you search for a full-time role.

## 5. Pay bills in the right order if you can't cover everything

1. Housing
2. Utilities
3. Food and medicine
4. Transportation you need for work or job searching
5. Secured debts (car loan)
6. Unsecured debts (credit cards). If you call these lenders and explain the job loss, they will often pause or reduce payments.

Payday loans and cash advances can make things worse. Their fees can quickly cost more than the original problem.
