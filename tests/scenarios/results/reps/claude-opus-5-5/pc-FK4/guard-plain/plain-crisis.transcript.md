### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. Here is a way to work through it.

## 1. Get the real numbers on paper

List these, monthly:

**Money coming in**
- Unemployment benefits (if you haven't applied yet, do it now. Claims often can't be backdated far.)
- Severance, side work, a partner's income
- Savings you could draw on, as a total

**Fixed costs**
- Rent
- Utilities, phone, internet
- Insurance (health, car)
- Minimum debt payments
- Transportation

**Variable costs**
- Groceries
- Gas
- Subscriptions
- Everything else (check the last 2–3 bank statements, which are more accurate than memory)

Then work out: **monthly shortfall = costs − income**, and **runway = savings ÷ shortfall**. Runway is how many months you have before things get critical.

## 2. Pay in priority order

When there isn't enough for everything, pay in this order:

1. **Housing.** Rent, or at least partial rent plus a conversation with the landlord.
2. **Utilities** you need: power, heat, water.
3. **Food.**
4. **Transportation and phone**, since you need both to find work.
5. **Insurance**, especially health.
6. **Debt.** Unsecured debt like credit cards and personal loans comes last. Missing those hurts your credit score. Missing rent can cost you your home.

## 3. Act on rent now, before you fall further behind

- **Talk to your landlord early.** Many will agree to a payment plan or partial payments rather than go through an eviction. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Look for rental assistance.** In the US, call **211** or go to 211.org for local emergency rent and utility programs. Local charities, religious groups and community action agencies also give one-time help.
- **Learn your tenant rights.** Eviction usually takes a legal process with notice periods. Local legal aid groups can explain your rights for free.

## 4. Cut costs and buy time

- Pause or cancel subscriptions.
- Call utility companies and ask about hardship programs or budget billing.
- Call lenders and card issuers and ask about hardship forbearance or deferral. Many have programs they don't advertise.
- Apply for food assistance (SNAP in the US) and look up local food banks. That frees up cash for rent.
- If you had employer health insurance, compare COBRA with a marketplace plan. Losing a job counts as a qualifying event, and marketplace plans are often much cheaper with subsidies.

## 5. If the gap is too big

If rent alone is more than your income will cover for a while, consider a cheaper place, a roommate, or temporarily moving in with family. Making that choice early usually goes better than being forced into it after an eviction.

---

If you share your numbers (income, rent, other main costs, savings) and roughly where you live, they can go into an actual budget with your runway worked out. A general location helps with finding specific assistance programs.
