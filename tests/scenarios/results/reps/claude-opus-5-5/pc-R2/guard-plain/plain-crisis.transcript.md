### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a lot of pressure at once. A clear picture of the numbers helps. Here is a way to set it up.

## Step 1: Get the real numbers

**Money coming in (monthly):**
- Unemployment benefits. If you haven't applied, apply now. Claims are often only backdated a short way.
- Severance or final pay
- Partner's or household income
- Side work or gig income
- Savings you can use, listed separately as a total

**Money going out.** Sort it into tiers:

| Tier | What goes here | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Survival | Missing these causes immediate harm | Rent, utilities, food, essential medication, minimum transport |
| 2. Obligations | Missing these causes damage, but it can be managed | Minimum debt payments, insurance, phone |
| 3. Flexible | Can be cut or paused | Subscriptions, eating out, memberships, non-essential shopping |

## Step 2: Find the gap

Survival + obligations − income = the monthly shortfall. Then divide your savings by the shortfall. That tells you roughly how many months you have, and it shows how urgent each next step is.

## Step 3: Close the gap from both sides

**Cut costs:**
- Cancel or pause everything in Tier 3.
- Call each lender, card issuer and utility. Ask for a hardship plan, deferral or reduced payment. Many have these, but you usually have to ask.
- Look at food assistance (SNAP or local food banks) and utility help (LIHEAP in the US, or the local equivalent).
- Check whether you qualify for Medicaid or subsidized insurance if you lost employer coverage.

**Deal with rent directly:**
- Contact your landlord **before** the payment is missed, or as soon as possible if it already has been. Offer a specific plan, such as a partial payment now and the rest by a set date. Landlords often prefer a plan to an eviction process.
- Search "[your city/county] emergency rental assistance". Also call 211 (US) or your local social services line. Charities such as the Salvation Army and Catholic Charities sometimes cover one month.
- If you receive any notice from your landlord, contact a local tenant rights organization or legal aid right away. Eviction rules and deadlines vary a lot by location.

**Add income:**
- Temp agencies, gig work or part-time work while you search. Check how this affects unemployment benefits where you live.
- Sell things you don't need.

## Step 4: Payment order if money runs short

1. Housing
2. Utilities
3. Food and medication
4. Transport needed for job searching or work
5. Secured debts (car loan)
6. Unsecured debts (credit cards). These are the most negotiable and the least urgent of the debts.

Avoid payday loans and high-interest cash advances if possible. They tend to make the gap larger.

---

If you share your actual figures (income, rent, other bills, savings) and roughly where you live, you can get a concrete budget and a list of assistance programs that apply to you. Approximate numbers are fine.
