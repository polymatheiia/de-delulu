### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. A clear picture of the numbers makes the next decisions easier. Here's a framework, followed by the numbers needed to make it specific.

## 1. Get the real numbers

**Money coming in (monthly):**
- Unemployment benefits (applied yet? Approved yet? Weekly amount?)
- Severance or final paycheck
- Savings you can draw from
- Any side income or help from others

**Money going out**, sorted into tiers:

| Tier | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Survival** | Rent, utilities, groceries, essential medications, phone, basic transport | Pay these first |
| **2. Protect** | Health insurance, minimum debt payments, car payment if you need the car | Pay these if possible, and negotiate the rest |
| **3. Pause** | Subscriptions, dining out, extra debt payments, retirement contributions | Cut or pause these now |

## 2. Work out your runway

**Runway = (savings + expected income) ÷ monthly survival costs.**

That tells you how many months you have. It decides how aggressive the cuts and housing decisions need to be.

## 3. Act on rent early

- **Contact your landlord before you miss a payment, or right away if you already have.** Ask for a payment plan, a partial payment, or a short deferral. Landlords often prefer this to an eviction, which costs them money and time.
- **Look for emergency rental assistance.** In the US, call or text **211** to reach local rent and utility programs. Charities like Salvation Army and Catholic Charities, plus local community action agencies, often run one-time rent help.
- **Learn your local eviction rules.** Eviction takes a formal legal process. Many areas have free tenant legal aid.

## 4. Lower other bills

- **Utilities:** Ask about hardship programs or budget billing. In the US, LIHEAP covers heating and cooling costs.
- **Debt:** Call your lenders and ask for hardship forbearance. Federal student loans have unemployment deferment.
- **Food:** SNAP and local food banks free up cash for rent.
- **Health insurance:** Losing a job is a qualifying event for marketplace coverage, and you may qualify for Medicaid at lower income.

## 5. If you can't close the gap

You'd look at a roommate, subletting a room, moving somewhere cheaper, or staying with family or friends for a while. It helps to decide this while you still have some runway, not after an eviction filing.

---

**To build an actual budget, these would help:**
1. Your monthly rent, and how much you're behind (if at all)
2. Other fixed bills: utilities, phone, insurance, car, debt minimums
3. Your savings and expected income, including unemployment status
4. Your country or state, since benefits and tenant protections vary a lot

Rough numbers are fine. With them, the runway and a month-by-month plan can be worked out.
