I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. Here is a way to work through it.

## Step 1: Get the numbers in one place

**Money coming in (monthly):**
- Unemployment benefits. If you haven't applied, do it now. Most places backdate only to the filing date.
- Severance, a final paycheck, or payout of unused vacation time
- Any side or part-time income
- A partner's or household income, if shared

**Cash on hand:** checking, savings, anything you can reach easily.

**Expenses, sorted into three tiers:**

| Tier | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Essentials** | Rent, utilities, groceries, essential transport, medications, phone (needed for the job search) |
| **2. Consequential** | Minimum debt payments, insurance, car payment |
| **3. Can pause** | Streaming, subscriptions, dining out, gym, extra debt payments |

## Step 2: Find the gap

**Monthly income − Tier 1 − Tier 2 = shortfall or surplus**

Then: **cash on hand ÷ monthly shortfall = months of runway.** That number shows how much time you have and how urgent the next steps are.

## Step 3: Rent

- **Contact your landlord before the rent is late, or as soon as possible if it already is.** Many landlords will agree to a partial payment or a payment plan. Eviction costs them money and time too. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Rental assistance:** call **211** (US) or search "[your city/county] emergency rental assistance." Local charities, churches, and community action agencies often have one-time rent funds.
- **If you get an eviction notice:** look up free legal aid in your area right away. Eviction rules and timelines vary a lot by location, and you often have more time and rights than the notice suggests.

## Step 4: Lower other costs

- **Food:** SNAP benefits and local food banks. This frees up cash for rent.
- **Utilities:** ask the utility company about hardship programs. LIHEAP helps with energy bills in the US.
- **Debt:** call your lenders and ask for hardship forbearance. Federal student loans have unemployment deferment and income-driven plans that can go as low as $0.
- **Health insurance:** losing a job is a qualifying event for Marketplace coverage. Medicaid may also apply at your new income.
- **Order of payment if you can't cover everything:** housing, utilities, food, and transport to work come before unsecured debt like credit cards.

## Next step

If you share rough figures (benefits, cash on hand, rent, and your other main bills), the math can be laid out with you, including what rent amount is realistic to offer your landlord. Your location (country, and state if you're in the US) also matters, because benefits and tenant protections differ a lot from place to place.
