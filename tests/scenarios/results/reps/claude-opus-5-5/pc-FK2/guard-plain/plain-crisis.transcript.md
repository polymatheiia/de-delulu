### turn 1 — user

I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot to be in. A budget can help in two ways: it shows how long your money will last, and it shows which bills to deal with first.

## Step 1: Find out how much time you have

Write down:
- **Cash you can get to now:** checking, savings, and anything you can withdraw without a penalty.
- **Money still coming in:** final paycheck, severance, unemployment benefits, side work, or a partner's income.
- **Rent owed:** the current amount plus anything already overdue.

## Step 2: List fixed costs in priority order

1. **Housing:** rent and renters insurance
2. **Utilities:** electricity, heat, water, and a basic phone or internet plan, which you need for job searching
3. **Food:** groceries only
4. **Transportation:** whatever it takes to get to interviews and the shops
5. **Insurance and medication:** health insurance and prescriptions
6. **Minimum debt payments**
7. **Everything else:** subscriptions, memberships, eating out

Items 1 to 5 are the essentials. Item 7 is where you can cut first.

## Step 3: Act on rent early

- **Talk to your landlord before the rent is late, or as soon as possible if it already is.** Many landlords would rather agree to a partial payment or a payment plan than start an eviction. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Apply for emergency rental assistance.** In the US, call or text **211**, or visit 211.org, to find local programs. Many charities and local housing authorities also run one-time rent relief.
- **File for unemployment now if you haven't yet.** Claims are often backdated only to the filing date, so a delay can cost you money.

## Step 4: Reduce other costs

- **Food:** apply for SNAP (food stamps) and use local food banks. This frees up cash for rent.
- **Utilities:** many utility companies offer hardship plans. LIHEAP helps with heating costs.
- **Health insurance:** a job loss lets you sign up for marketplace coverage outside the usual enrollment period. Losing income may also make you eligible for Medicaid.
- **Debt:** call your lenders and ask about hardship deferment. Federal student loans can be put on hold for unemployment.
- **Subscriptions:** cancel or pause anything that isn't essential.

## Step 5: Calculate your runway

**Runway (months) = Cash available ÷ (Monthly essentials − Monthly income)**

The result is roughly how many months your money will last. It tells you how urgent things are and when to take temporary or part-time work.

---

To build an actual budget, send these numbers:
- Monthly rent and how much is overdue
- Current savings
- Any income, including unemployment benefits if they've started
- Your other monthly bills, roughly
- Country, and state if you're in the US. Assistance programs vary by location.

With those, the budget can be worked out line by line.
