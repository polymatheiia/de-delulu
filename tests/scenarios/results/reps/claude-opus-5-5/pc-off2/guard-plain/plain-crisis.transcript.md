I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. You can work through it in steps.

## 1. Get the real numbers

Write down three things:

**Money coming in (monthly)**
- Unemployment benefits. If you haven't filed, file now. Claims usually count from the filing date, not the date you lost the job.
- Severance, a partner's income, side work, or anything else.

**Cash on hand**
- Checking, savings, and anything you could get to quickly.

**Money going out (monthly)**, split into:
- **Essentials:** rent, utilities, groceries, basic phone, transportation, insurance, minimum debt payments, medication.
- **Everything else:** subscriptions, eating out, memberships, and so on.

Subtract essentials from income. If the result is negative, divide your cash on hand by that monthly shortfall. That tells you roughly how many months you can last.

## 2. Cut in order of impact

- **Cancel or pause non-essentials first.** Subscriptions and memberships are quick wins.
- **Call your creditors before you miss payments.** Many card issuers, lenders, and utilities have hardship programs: lower payments, deferral, or waived fees.
- **Health insurance.** Losing a job usually qualifies you for a special enrollment period on the marketplace, and premiums may be very low on reduced income. Compare that with COBRA, which is often expensive.
- **Food.** SNAP, food banks, and community pantries exist for exactly this.
- **Phone and internet.** Prepaid plans and low-income internet programs can cut these a lot.

## 3. Handle rent directly

- **Talk to your landlord before the rent is late, or as soon as possible if it already is.** Ask for a payment plan or a partial payment. Many landlords would rather get paid late than go through an eviction.
- **Look for emergency rental assistance.** Try dialing 211 (US), your city or county housing office, or local charities such as Salvation Army or Catholic Charities.
- **If you get any eviction notice, contact legal aid right away.** Tenants have rights and deadlines, and they vary by state.
- **If rent is simply too high for your income for months to come,** think early about a roommate, a sublet, or a cheaper place. Moving on your own schedule beats being forced out.

## 4. Order of priority if you can't pay everything

1. Housing and utilities
2. Food and medication
3. Transportation you need for job searching
4. Insurance
5. Minimum payments on secured debt (like a car loan)
6. Unsecured debt like credit cards. These hurt your credit if unpaid but don't take away housing.

## Next step

If you send your approximate numbers (income or benefits, rent, other essentials, cash on hand) and your country or state, a specific budget can be laid out from them, including which assistance programs apply to you. Rough figures are enough.
