I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. A plan can be built from what you have now. Here is a framework to start, then the numbers needed to make it specific.

## 1. Get the full picture

**Money available:**
- Cash and savings
- Final paycheck, severance, or unused PTO payout
- Unemployment benefits (if you haven't applied, do it now; there is usually a waiting period)
- Any side income, partner income, or support

**Monthly costs, split into two groups:**
- **Essential:** rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, phone, minimum debt payments, transportation, medication
- **Flexible:** subscriptions, eating out, memberships, non-urgent purchases

## 2. Work out your runway
Runway = money available ÷ essential monthly costs. That number shows how many months you can cover and how urgent each step below is.

## 3. Cut costs, biggest first
- Cancel or pause flexible spending, starting with subscriptions.
- Call providers (phone, internet, insurance) and ask about hardship plans or cheaper tiers.
- Student loans: ask about income-driven repayment or forbearance.
- Credit cards: ask about hardship programs. Pay minimums only and keep cash on hand.

## 4. Deal with rent directly
- **Contact your landlord early**, before you miss a payment if you can. Many prefer a payment plan to an eviction. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Emergency rental assistance:** call 211 (US) or search "[your city/county] rental assistance." Local programs, charities, and religious organizations often cover one or two months.
- **Know your tenant rights:** local tenant unions or legal aid can tell you what notice your landlord must give.
- If rent is unaffordable over the longer term, compare options like a roommate, subletting a room, or moving somewhere cheaper when the lease ends.

## 5. Free up money for other essentials
- Food banks and SNAP (food assistance) let your cash go further on rent.
- Utility assistance programs (LIHEAP in the US) help with heating and electricity bills.
- If you lost health insurance, job loss qualifies you for a special enrollment period, and Medicaid may apply at your current income.

## 6. Order of payment if money runs short
1. Housing and utilities
2. Food and medication
3. Transportation needed for job searching
4. Insurance
5. Minimum debt payments

Unsecured debt like credit cards comes after housing and food.

---

To build an actual budget, send:
- Monthly rent and how far behind you are, if at all
- Your other monthly bills, roughly
- Savings or cash on hand
- Whether you're getting unemployment, and how much
- Your country or state, since assistance programs vary

Rough numbers are enough to start.
