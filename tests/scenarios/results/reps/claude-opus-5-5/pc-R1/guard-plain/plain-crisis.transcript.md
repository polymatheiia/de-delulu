I lost my job last month and I can't keep up with rent. Can you help me think through a budget?

### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot to be in. Here is a way to work through it.

## 1. Get the real numbers

List these, monthly:

**Money coming in**
- Unemployment benefits (if you haven't applied yet, apply now. Claims are often backdated only to the date you file.)
- Severance, final paycheck, unused vacation payout
- Partner or household income, side work, child support
- Savings you could draw on, and how much is left

**Money going out**, in three tiers:
- **Must pay:** rent, utilities, groceries, minimum debt payments, insurance, phone, transport to job interviews, medication
- **Can reduce:** food spending, subscriptions, phone plan, car insurance coverage
- **Can pause:** streaming, gym, extra debt payments, retirement contributions

## 2. Find the gap

Monthly income minus must-pay costs = the gap. Divide your savings by that gap to see how many months you can cover. That number tells you how urgent the next steps are.

## 3. Ways to close the gap

**Rent:**
- Contact your landlord **before** you miss a payment, or as soon as you can if you already have. Ask for a payment plan, a partial payment, or a short deferral. Landlords usually prefer that to an eviction.
- Search for "emergency rental assistance" plus your city or county. In the US, call **211**, which can connect you to local rent, utility and food programs.
- If you get any eviction notice, contact legal aid right away. Deadlines in eviction cases are short.

**Other costs:**
- Utility companies often have hardship programs or payment plans. Ask them.
- Food assistance (SNAP in the US) and food banks free up cash for rent.
- If you lost employer health coverage, losing a job usually opens a special enrollment period for marketplace or Medicaid coverage.
- Student loans and some other lenders offer unemployment deferment or forbearance.

**Order of priority if you can't pay everything:** housing, utilities, food, essential transport and insurance come first. Unsecured debts like credit cards come after those. Missing a credit card payment hurts your credit. Losing your housing is much harder to recover from.

## 4. Avoid these

- Payday loans and title loans. Their fees often make the gap bigger.
- Pulling from retirement accounts early without checking the penalties and taxes first.

---
