### turn 1 — assistant

Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot. Here's a way to work through it.

## Step 1: Get the real numbers

Write down:

**Money coming in (monthly)**
- Unemployment benefits. If you haven't applied yet, apply now. Most places pay from the date you file, not the date you lost the job.
- Severance, last paycheck, or accrued PTO payout
- Any partner or household income, side work, or child support
- Savings you can draw on (total amount, kept separate)

**Money going out, sorted by priority**
1. **Housing:** rent and utilities (electric, heat, water)
2. **Food:** groceries only
3. **Essentials:** phone/internet (needed for the job search), transport, medications, insurance
4. **Minimum debt payments:** car loan, credit cards, student loans
5. **Everything else:** subscriptions, dining out, memberships

## Step 2: Find the gap

Income minus essentials (tiers 1–3) gives your monthly shortfall. Divide savings by that shortfall to see how many months you can last. That number tells you how urgent the next steps are.

## Step 3: Cut and defer

- **Cancel or pause** everything in tier 5 now.
- **Student loans:** federal loans in the US allow unemployment deferment or income-driven repayment, which can be $0.
- **Credit cards and car loans:** call and ask for a hardship program. Many lenders offer reduced payments for a few months.
- **Utilities:** ask about hardship plans. LIHEAP in the US helps with heating and cooling bills.
- **Health insurance:** losing a job is a qualifying event. You can enroll in a marketplace plan or Medicaid outside the normal enrollment window. That deadline is usually 60 days, so check it soon.
- **Food:** SNAP and local food banks. Using them for groceries frees up cash for rent.

## Step 4: Deal with rent directly

- **Talk to your landlord before the rent is late, or as soon as possible if it already is.** Many landlords will agree to a payment plan, because evicting someone costs them money too. Get any agreement in writing.
- **Emergency rental assistance:** call 211 (US) or your local housing authority. Charities like Salvation Army and Catholic Charities, along with local churches, often give one-time rent help.
- **Know your rights:** eviction rules vary a lot by location. A local tenant union or free legal aid office can tell you what notice is required and how long the process takes. If you receive any eviction paperwork, do not ignore it. Respond and show up to any court date.

## Step 5: If the numbers still don't work

The options are more income (temp or gig work, selling items you don't need), lower housing costs (a roommate, subletting, or moving somewhere cheaper when your lease allows), or bridge help from family.
