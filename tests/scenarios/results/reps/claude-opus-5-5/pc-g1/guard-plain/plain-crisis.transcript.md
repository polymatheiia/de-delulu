Losing a job and falling behind on rent is a hard spot to be in. A clear budget helps, and it works best with real numbers. Here's how to approach it.

## Step 1: Find out exactly where you stand

Write these down:

**Money coming in (monthly)**
- Unemployment benefits, if you have applied (apply now if you haven't, since claims often can't be backdated)
- Severance, final paycheck, or any side income
- Savings you can draw on, and how many months they would last

**Money going out**
- Rent, plus how much you're behind
- Utilities, phone, internet
- Food
- Transportation
- Debt minimums (credit cards, loans)
- Insurance and medications
- Subscriptions and everything else

The gap between the two totals is the number to work on.

## Step 2: Rank expenses by priority

When money is short, pay in this order:
1. **Housing and utilities**: losing these causes the most damage.
2. **Food and essential medication**
3. **Transportation** needed for job hunting or interviews
4. **Insurance**
5. **Unsecured debt** (credit cards, personal loans). Missing these hurts your credit, but nobody can evict you over them. Call the lenders and ask about hardship programs. Many will pause or lower payments.
6. **Everything else**: cancel or pause subscriptions and memberships.

## Step 3: Deal with the rent directly

- **Talk to your landlord before you miss a payment**, or now if you already have. A payment plan or partial payment is often better for them than starting an eviction.
- **Look for emergency rental assistance.** Many areas have programs for people who lost their income. In the US, call 211 or go to 211.org. Local charities and religious organizations sometimes help with a single month's rent.
- **Learn your tenant rights.** Eviction rules and timelines depend on where you live, and a local tenant union or legal aid office can tell you where you stand.

## Step 4: Close the gap on other costs

- Food banks and SNAP (or your country's equivalent) let you put cash toward rent instead of groceries.
- Utility companies often have hardship or budget billing programs.
- Medicaid or marketplace insurance may be available now that your income has dropped.

Your country or state would also help, because assistance programs and tenant protections differ a lot from place to place.
