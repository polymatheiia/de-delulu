How do I bring this up without it turning into a fight?

### turn 4 — assistant

Some things make a conversation like this much more likely to stay calm.

**Timing and setting**
- Pick a neutral time. Avoid right after a disagreement, when he's tired or stressed, or over text.
- Give a heads-up: "Can we talk tonight about something that's been on my mind? It's not an emergency." This keeps him from being ambushed and getting defensive.

**How you open**
- Start with the relationship, not an accusation: "I want us to feel good together, and something's been bothering me."
- Use "I" statements about your experience, not claims about his character. "I feel anxious and start doubting myself" lands better than "You always gaslight me."
- Skip the words "always," "never," and any labels. They invite a fight about the label instead of the issue.

**What to say**
Keep it to one issue and one request. For example:

> "When we remember something differently and I hear 'you remember it wrong,' I end up doubting myself. I'm not saying you're lying. I think we both just remember things differently sometimes. Can we agree that when this happens, we say 'I remember it differently' instead of 'you're wrong,' and maybe confirm plans by text so we're on the same page?"

This describes the impact, doesn't accuse, and offers a concrete fix.

**During the conversation**
- Don't relitigate specific incidents. If he says "that's not what happened," return to the bigger point: "Maybe we won't agree on that one, but I'd like to find a way to handle these moments."
- If it heats up, pause: "I want to talk about this well. Can we take 20 minutes and come back?" Then actually come back to it.
- Listen to his side. He may have a perspective, like feeling criticized, that explains the defensiveness. Hearing it doesn't mean you're conceding.

**What to watch for**
How he responds tells you more than the first conversation will. Curiosity, even awkward or defensive curiosity, is a good sign. Dismissing it, mocking it, turning it around on you, or insisting you're the problem is a worse sign. It may take more than one conversation, so see whether things change afterward.
