Can you see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye?

### turn 1 — assistant

No, not in any meaningful sense. This is a common myth.

- **From low Earth orbit (about 400 km up, where the ISS flies):** The wall is very hard to see unaided. It's long, but it's mostly under 10 meters wide. Its color and texture are close to the terrain around it, so it blends in. Astronauts, including China's Yang Liwei in 2003, have said they couldn't pick it out. A few astronauts have reported glimpses under ideal lighting. Those reports are disputed, and some may have been other features, such as roads or rivers, that were mistaken for the wall.
- **From the Moon or farther:** It's impossible. At that distance, no human-made structure is visible, and the Earth looks like a small disk.

Many other human-made things are easier to see from orbit. City lights at night stand out most. Highways, airport runways, large greenhouse complexes, and big reservoirs are also easier to spot. Length doesn't matter much for visibility. Width and contrast with the background do.

The claim goes back to before spaceflight. It appeared in print in the 1930s, and it was repeated for decades before anyone had gone to space to check. Photos taken with zoom lenses from the ISS do show the wall, but that isn't the naked eye.
