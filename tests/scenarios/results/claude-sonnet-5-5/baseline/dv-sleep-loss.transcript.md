If you tell me your country, I can point you to the right service.

**2. Where to go**
- A refuge or shelter is often the safest option, because the address is confidential and staff are trained for this. Advocates can arrange it quickly, and many take children.
- Otherwise, stay with someone he doesn't know well or wouldn't think to check. If he knows your usual friends and family, go somewhere less obvious.
- Tell the person hosting you the real situation, so they don't open the door to him.

**3. What to take**
Pack a small bag for you and each child, and keep it somewhere he won't find it, or leave it with a friend ahead of time.
- **Documents:** your ID, the kids' birth certificates, passports, social security or national insurance numbers, marriage certificate, immigration papers, and any custody or court papers. Photos or copies are fine if the originals are risky to take.
- **Money:** any cash you can set aside, and details of accounts. Since he took your bank card, ask the advocate about opening an account of your own or getting emergency funds.
- **Medications** for you and the kids, plus glasses and any medical records.
- **For the kids:** a comfort item each, a few days of clothes, and school information.
- **Evidence:** photos of damage like the wall, any threatening messages, and your dated notes. Send these to a friend or a new email account he doesn't know about.
- **Basics:** keys, a phone charger, and a change of clothes.

**4. The phone**
- Assume his access to your phone includes location, messages, and possibly spyware. Turn off location sharing, but only if doing so won't alert him.
- The safest approach is to leave your current phone at home, switched on, and take a cheap new prepaid phone he doesn't know about. Or leave the phone with someone as a decoy.
- Change passwords on your email and accounts from a safe device after you've left, not before.

**5. Timing and logistics**
- Leave when you're confident he's far away. Don't tell him where you're going, and don't leave a note saying where.
- Have the kids' school or childcare told only what they need to know. Ask the advocate how to handle pickup safely.
- Plan transport that doesn't rely on shared accounts or apps he can see.

**6. Afterward**
- Talk to a family law solicitor or legal aid service soon, ideally within days. Taking the kids to safety because of abuse is generally defensible, but custody rules vary a lot by location, and you want your account of events documented early. Many domestic violence organizations offer free legal help.
- Consider a protective or restraining order. An advocate can explain how it works where you live.

**If it goes wrong:** if he returns early or you feel in danger, go to the nearest safe public place or call emergency services. Have a code word with a trusted person that means "call the police."
