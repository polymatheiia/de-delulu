### turn 1 — user

What's the current population estimate of Poland, and where does the figure come from?

### turn 1 — assistant

**Poland's population is about 37.2 million**, according to Statistics Poland (GUS), the national statistics office.

- **End of June 2026:** 37.243 million, down about 159,000 from a year earlier.
- **End of Q1 2026:** 37.28 million (preliminary), down about 155,000 year on year.
- **Cause of the decline:** Deaths exceed births. Roughly 115,000 births were recorded in the first half of 2026, and natural change was about -54,500.

**Where the figure comes from:** GUS estimates the population each quarter. It starts from the last census base and adds births, deaths and migration flows. The results are published in GUS's "Sytuacja społeczno-gospodarcza kraju" (socio-economic situation) reports and in its territorial-profile publications. Polish and international news outlets then report them.

Other sources give different numbers:
- **GUS register-based series:** This experimental series draws on administrative records. It gives roughly 38.8 million for the end of 2025, which is higher than the standard estimate.
- **UN World Population Prospects 2024:** It projects about 37.84 million for mid-2026. That is a model projection, not a count.

For the official current figure, use the GUS estimate of about 37.2 million.

### turn 2 — user

And what was it in 2000? Give me the source for that one too.

### turn 2 — assistant

Poland's population in 2000 was about **38.25 million**. I couldn't confirm that figure against a primary GUS source, so treat it as approximate.

- **Best match:** [Population of Poland 2000 – PopulationPyramid](https://www.populationpyramid.net/poland/2000/) lists 38,258,071. That is a UN-based modeled figure for mid-year, not a GUS count.
- **Conflicting figure:** The Wikipedia demographics table gives 38,556,693 for 2000, about 300,000 higher. It doesn't say what basis it uses.
- **Not found:** The search didn't return the GUS end-of-2000 table. GUS's own figures would be the authoritative source, and they are probably close to 38.25 million.

Comparing 2000 with today, the population has fallen by roughly 1 million (about 38.25 million to 37.24 million). Part of that comparison mixes bases, because 2000 is a UN mid-year figure and 2026 is a GUS end-of-June estimate. The real difference could be a few hundred thousand either way.

For a definitive 2000 number, look up the GUS annual population tables for 31 December 2000 at stat.gov.pl.
