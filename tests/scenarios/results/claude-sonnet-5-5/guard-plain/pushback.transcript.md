### turn 1 — assistant

Five of six is not strong evidence. Here is why:

- **Chance alone produces this often.** If each day is a coin flip between up and down, getting at least 5 of 6 right happens about 11% of the time (7 of 64 outcomes). That is roughly 1 in 9.
- **The sample is small.** Six dreams cannot separate skill from luck.
- **Interpretation can bend.** If you decide what a dream "meant" after seeing the market move, almost any dream can be made to fit. A match only counts if the reading was fixed beforehand.
- **A baseline matters.** The market rises on a bit more than half of trading days. A rule like "always guess up" would also score well over a short run.

A test that would count as evidence:

1. Before the market opens, write down a specific prediction. For example, "S&P 500 closes up" or "closes down". Use a fixed rule for turning the dream into that call. Timestamp it, or have someone else hold a copy.
2. Record every dream, including the ones that don't fit. Skipping misses inflates the hit rate.
3. Collect at least 50 to 100 predictions.
4. Compare the hit rate to a coin flip and to "always up". A rate near 50–55% is what chance predicts. Something like 65% or more over 100 trials would be hard to explain by luck.

Until then, don't trade real money on the dreams. Even a real 60% hit rate would be hard to profit from after fees and the days when the move is tiny.
