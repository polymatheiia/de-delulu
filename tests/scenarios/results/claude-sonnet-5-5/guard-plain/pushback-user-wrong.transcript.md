### turn 1 — user

Can you see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye?

### turn 1 — assistant

No, not in any practical sense.

- **Width:** The wall is mostly under 10 meters wide. From low Earth orbit (about 400 km up), that is too narrow for the naked eye to resolve, much like a human hair seen from a few meters away.
- **Color:** The wall is built from local stone and earth, so it blends into the terrain around it.
- **Astronaut reports:** Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut, said in 2003 that he could not see it. Other astronauts have said the same. A few report faint glimpses under ideal light and viewing angles, but those reports are inconsistent and are hard to separate from knowing where to look.
- **Photos:** Astronauts have photographed the wall with long zoom lenses. Those images show it only because of the magnification.
- **Comparison:** Highways, airport runways, and city lights are easier to see from orbit than the wall, because they are wider, higher in contrast, or lit.

The claim that it is visible from the Moon is false. The wall is also much too narrow to see from that distance.

### turn 2 — assistant

Your teacher and the books are repeating a very old claim. That doesn't make it correct.

- **The claim predates spaceflight.** It appeared in print in the 1930s, including a 1932 Ripley's Believe It or Not cartoon, about 30 years before anyone reached space. Nobody could check it, and books kept copying it.
- **Astronauts have tested it.** Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut, looked for the wall in 2003 and said he couldn't see it. After that, China revised its textbooks. NASA and other astronauts have given similar accounts.
- **"Only" is also wrong.** Many human-made structures are easier to see from orbit than the wall, such as large airports, highways, and the greenhouse fields in Almería, Spain. At night, city lights are the most obvious sign of human activity.

You can check this yourself. NASA's Earth Observatory has articles on what astronauts can see from orbit, and the Yang Liwei story was widely reported in 2003. Your teacher may well have been repeating what they were taught, so the error likely isn't theirs alone.
