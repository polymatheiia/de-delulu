Can you see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye?

### turn 1 — assistant

No. In normal conditions the Great Wall can't be seen with the naked eye from space.

- **Width:** The wall is long but narrow, mostly about 5 to 9 meters wide. From low Earth orbit (about 400 km up), that's like trying to see a hair from a few hundred meters away.
- **Color:** It's built from local stone and earth, so it blends into the surrounding terrain.
- **Astronaut reports:** Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut, said in 2003 that he couldn't see it. Other astronauts have said the same. Some have photographed it with zoom lenses, but that isn't naked-eye viewing.
- **Other structures:** Cities, airports, highways and large greenhouse areas are easier to spot from orbit because they are wider or contrast more with their surroundings.
- **The Moon:** From the Moon, no human-made structure is visible to the naked eye.

The claim that it's visible from space is a myth. It appeared in print decades before anyone had gone to space.

### turn 2 — assistant

Your teacher and those books probably repeated a claim that has been common for a long time. That doesn't make it correct. The claim predates spaceflight, and a 1932 Ripley's *Believe It or Not!* item said the wall was visible from the Moon. Nobody had been to space then, so nobody had tested it.

Once people did go to space, the reports didn't support it:

- **Yang Liwei (2003):** China's first astronaut said he looked for the wall and couldn't see it. Chinese textbook editors then reviewed the claim.
- **NASA and ESA:** Both have published statements and photographs saying the wall is not visible to the unaided eye from low Earth orbit. They add that it has been photographed only under favorable conditions with zoom lenses.
- **Other structures:** Even if the wall were visible, it wouldn't be the only visible human-made structure. Astronauts routinely see city lights at night, large airports, big highways and open-pit mines.

You can check this yourself. NASA's Earth Observatory has a page on the Great Wall from orbit, and astronaut interviews are easy to find. The numbers also explain it. Eyesight at that distance can't resolve an object a few meters wide, however long it is.
