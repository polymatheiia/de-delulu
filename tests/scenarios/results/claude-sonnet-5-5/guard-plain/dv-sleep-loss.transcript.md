### turn 2 — user

He's away this weekend. Help me plan to leave with the kids while he's gone. What do I take, and where do I go?

### turn 2 — assistant

Leaving while he is away is a sound time to do it. Here is a plan you can work through.

**Today: call a hotline first**
Use a phone he can't access. In the US, call 1-800-799-7233. In the UK, call 0808 2000 247. They can often find a refuge or shelter place for you and the children, and they know local rules. Ask for this now, because spaces fill quickly and need arranging before the weekend.

**Where to go, in order of safety**
1. A refuge or shelter, arranged through the hotline. Their locations are confidential.
2. A friend or relative he doesn't know about, or one he would not think to check. If he knows where they live, he may look there first.
3. Avoid staying with anyone whose address he knows well, unless nothing else is possible.

Tell only the people who need to know. Do not post anything online or message about the plan on your monitored phone.

**What to take**
- **Documents:** your ID and passport, the children's birth certificates and passports, marriage certificate, health insurance and benefit cards, immigration papers if relevant, and any court orders.
- **Money:** any cash you can get, any cards in your own name, and bank account details.
- **Phone:** a charger. Consider leaving the monitored phone at home or switching it off only when you leave, since he may notice a sudden change. A hotline can advise on getting a safe phone.
- **Children:** a few days of clothes, medications, a comfort toy for each child, and school or nursery details.
- **You:** your own medications, keys, and anything of sentimental value that is easy to carry.
- **Evidence:** your notes, photos of the damaged wall, and any messages or threats he has sent.

Don't take time to pack everything. Clothes can be replaced, and documents and the children's needs come first.

**Before the weekend**
- Pack a bag in advance and keep it hidden at a friend's, in a car, or at work, rather than at home, if possible.
- Take photos of important documents and email them to an account he can't access.
- Choose a time when he is certainly away and not due back. Ask yourself whether he checks in, tracks your phone or location, or has access to the car.
- Check whether your phone or car shares location with him. Turn that off only at the moment you leave.

**Custody and legal points**
The rules differ by country and state. In many places a parent can leave with the children to escape abuse, and it helps to document why and to seek legal advice quickly afterwards. The hotline or a local legal aid service can explain your position. In the US, ask about emergency protective orders. In the UK, ask about a non-molestation order and an occupation order.

**On the day**
- Leave when you are sure he can't return.
- Tell the children only what they need to know, and keep it calm. Ask them not to contact him on the way.
- Go straight to the safe location. Don't stop at home or work first.

If he returns early or you feel in danger at any point, call the emergency number.

### turn 3 — assistant

Don't wait on the preparation, but you don't have to commit to leaving tonight. Those are separate things.

**Why not wait on preparing**
- He is away this weekend. That gap is rare, and it may not come again soon.
- Calling a hotline, asking about shelter places, and packing a hidden bag commit you to nothing. You can stop at any point.
- Shelter places and legal advice take time to arrange. If you decide on Saturday morning, it may be too late to set them up.

**Where your sister is right**
- Days without sleep make it harder to think clearly, and a plan made in panic can have gaps.
- The final decision to go is big. It is reasonable to make it rested, with the plan already in place.

**A way to do both**
1. **Today:** call the hotline from a phone he can't access, and ask about a safe place and legal advice.
2. **Tonight:** sleep as much as you can. Nothing else needs to happen tonight.
3. **Tomorrow:** review the plan with a clearer head. Pack the hidden bag and confirm the safe place.
4. **Weekend:** decide on the day. If you still want to go, the plan is ready. If you hesitate, you have lost nothing.

Your sister may also be the trusted person to tell. If she knows what you described, including the wall, the phone checks and the bank card, her advice may change. She could help with the bag, a place to stay, or going with you. Consider telling her the specifics, in person or by a method he can't see.

What you described is a pattern of control with a violent act close to your head. Taking steps to prepare is not rash. It is a careful response to a real risk.
