### turn 1 — user

Is it possible for a neighbour to listen through walls using wifi signals?

### turn 1 — assistant

Not in any practical way.

**What WiFi signals can do through walls:**
- Research groups have shown that WiFi can detect motion, presence, breathing, and rough body pose through walls. Examples are MIT's Wi-Vi and Carnegie Mellon's WiFi-based pose estimation.
- This works by analyzing how people disturb the signal (channel state information). It needs specialized hardware or firmware, and usually a lab setup.

**What it can't do:**
- Ordinary 2.4/5 GHz WiFi does not carry sound. Speech makes surfaces vibrate by tiny amounts, far below what standard WiFi can resolve.
- Some research uses millimeter-wave radar (60 GHz or higher) to recover audio from vibrating objects. These systems use dedicated radar hardware, short range, and controlled conditions. They are not ordinary WiFi, and no known consumer tool does this through a wall.

**Realistic risks from a neighbour:**
- Someone with a custom setup could infer whether you're home or moving around. That is far less than hearing you.
- Thin shared walls leak sound directly. That is a much more likely way to be overheard.
- A compromised device in your home, such as a phone, smart speaker, or camera, is a far likelier way for audio to be captured.

**If you want to reduce exposure:**
- Use WPA2 or WPA3 with a strong password.
- Keep router and device firmware updated.
- Check which devices are connected to your network.
- Disable microphones you don't use.

If something specific made you ask, such as a neighbour behaving oddly or a device acting strangely, tell me what you noticed and I can help work out what it likely is.

### turn 2 — user

A new neighbour moved in three months ago. Since then my phone battery drains fast and I hear clicks on calls. Coincidence?

### turn 2 — assistant

Those symptoms are common and have ordinary causes. Both starting around the time the neighbour arrived is a timing overlap, and it doesn't show a connection. Nothing in what you describe points to the neighbour.

**Fast battery drain**
- Battery wear. Lithium batteries lose capacity noticeably after 1-3 years.
- Weak signal. A phone searching for signal uses much more power.
- A recent OS update, which can cause a few days of heavy background activity.
- One or more apps running in the background.
- Cold or hot conditions. Seasonal change matters, and it is now November.

**Clicks on calls**
- Poor signal, or handoffs between cell towers or between WiFi calling and cellular.
- Carrier network problems or congestion.
- A faulty headset, case, or microphone contact.
- Interference from nearby electronics.
- Clicking was a common sign of old wiretaps, but it isn't a reliable sign of one today. Modern calls are digital and packet-based, so clicks and glitches usually come from the network.

**Ways to check**
1. Open the battery settings. Look at battery health, and see which apps use the most power. If the drain comes from known apps or from "cell standby," the cause is mundane.
2. Check for unfamiliar apps and for apps with device-administrator rights. On Android, run Google's Safety Check or Play Protect. On iPhone, review Settings > Privacy & Security.
3. Install pending updates and restart the phone.
4. Test the same SIM in another phone, or another SIM in your phone. If the clicks follow the SIM or the carrier, the problem is on the network side.
5. Ask your carrier about line quality in your area.
6. A phone repair shop can test the battery. If you still suspect something, a reputable mobile security service can check the device.

If a factory reset and a fresh battery test don't change anything, that would be worth a closer look. Until then, the evidence points to wear, signal, or carrier issues.
