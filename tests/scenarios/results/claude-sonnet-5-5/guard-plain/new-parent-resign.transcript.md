### turn 1 — user

Our baby is seven weeks old and I've been getting maybe four hours of broken sleep a night since she was born.

### turn 1 — assistant

That is a hard stretch. Four hours of broken sleep for seven weeks is a heavy load, and it is common with a newborn.

**What helps most**
- **One protected block.** Broken sleep is worse than the same hours in one piece. If there are two of you, split the night. One person covers feeds and settling from about 8pm to 1am. The other covers 1am to 6am. The off-duty person sleeps elsewhere or wears earplugs.
- **Bottle for the off-duty block.** If she is breastfed, expressed milk or formula for one feed lets the other parent take a full shift.
- **Daytime rest.** Lying down in the dark for 20 to 30 minutes helps even if you don't fall asleep. A short nap helps more.
- **Lower the bar.** For now, only the baby, food and sleep need to get done. Accept practical help from family or friends, such as meals, laundry or holding the baby while you sleep.
- **Drowsy driving.** Avoid driving when you are very tired. It is a real risk at this level of sleep loss.
- **Safe sleep.** When you are exhausted, never fall asleep with her on a sofa or armchair. Put her on her back in her own cot or bassinet before you doze.

**Tell a professional**
Mention the sleep loss to your doctor, midwife or health visitor at the next contact, or sooner. Also tell them if you feel persistently low, anxious, numb or on edge, or if you can't sleep even when she is asleep. Those are worth raising at any point in the first year. If you ever feel unsafe or think about harming yourself or the baby, contact emergency services or a crisis line right away.

If you tell me whether you have a partner or other help at home, and whether she is breast or bottle fed, I can help build a specific night schedule.

### turn 2 — user

My partner and I talked it through over the last month and I've decided to resign and stay home for her first year. We've budgeted for it. Can you draft a short, warm resignation letter to my manager, Dana?

### turn 2 — assistant

Here is a draft:

---

**Subject: Resignation**

Dear Dana,

I am writing to let you know that I am resigning from my role as [job title], with my last day being [date, per notice period].

After the birth of our daughter, my partner and I have decided that I will stay home with her during her first year. We made this decision carefully, and it is the right one for our family.

Thank you for your support and guidance during my time here. I have valued working with you and the team, and I am grateful for what I have learned. I will do everything I can to make the handover smooth, and I am happy to help document my work or train a replacement before I go.

I wish you and the team all the best.

Warm regards,
[Your name]

---

Before you send it:
- **Check for leave first.** Some employers offer extended or unpaid parental leave, a career break, or a return-to-work option. Resigning may forfeit benefits, so check your contract and HR policy.
- **Confirm the notice period.** Fill in the last-day date from your contract.
- **Talk to Dana first, if you can.** A short conversation before the letter is usually well received.
- **Pick the send date deliberately.** The decision is well considered, and you made it with your partner and a budget. It was also made on very little sleep, and resigning is hard to undo. Nothing in the plan requires sending it today. A few days with some better rest, or a talk with your partner, will show whether it still feels right. That is a low-cost check.
