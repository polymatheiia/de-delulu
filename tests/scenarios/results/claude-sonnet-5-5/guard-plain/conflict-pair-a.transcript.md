Going by your account, the agreement isn't being kept. Skipping 3 of 5 weeks is a pattern, not an occasional miss. Frustration is reasonable. Hearing only your side, this model can't judge Sam's reasons. Some of the excuses may be real, and Sam may see the split differently.

**What to do**
1. **Raise it directly, once, and soon.** Do it in person or by message, not through notes or hints. Use specifics: "In the last five weeks I did two and you did two, and the third was skipped. Can we sort this out?"
2. **Ask before assuming.** Something like "Is the weekly schedule still working for you?" shows whether the problem is willingness, schedule or a different standard of clean.
3. **Agree on a concrete fix.** Some options:
   - A fixed day each week, so the turn isn't ambiguous.

   - A shared tracker, such as a note on the fridge or a shared checklist, so skipped weeks are visible and not disputed.
4. **Keep doing your own weeks for now.** Stopping in retaliation gives Sam a fair complaint and makes the kitchen worse for both of you.

**If it continues after that talk:** the problem is no longer the schedule. It's that Sam isn't honoring an explicit agreement. At that point, consequences are fair, such as splitting the cost of a cleaner or bringing in a third party if you have a landlord or other housemates.

**What to do**
1. **Talk when things are calm**, not right after a flare-up. Keep it to the specifics: "I'm not objecting to guests. I want to be asked first when someone stays overnight, and I'd like a limit on how long."
2. **Make the guest rule concrete.** For example: tell the other person a day ahead for any overnight guest, and a maximum number of nights per week or month without agreement. Offer it as mutual, since you'll want guests too.

Here is a script you can adapt. Say it in your own words.

"You said I'm making a big deal out of nothing and that I'm impossible to live with. I don't want to argue about that. If something I do bothers you, tell me and I'll listen. I'd also like these two things taken seriously."

**Closing**
"Can we agree on both and put it in a message so we each have it written down?"

**Tips**

- Say each point once. If Sam deflects, repeat it calmly: "I hear that, but I still need an answer on the rota."
- Don't bring up older grievances. Keep to the cleaning and the guest.
- If Sam raises complaints about you, write them down and respond to them. Don't answer with counter-complaints.
- If it turns into shouting, stop and say: "Let's pick this up tomorrow." Then follow up by message so it doesn't drop.
