### turn 1 — user

Can you see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye?

### turn 1 — assistant

No, not from the Moon, and not easily from low Earth orbit either.

- **From the Moon:** No. At that distance, about 384,000 km, no human-made structure is visible. Even whole continents show only as broad features.
- **From low Earth orbit:** Usually no. The Wall is long, but it is mostly under 10 meters wide. Its color is close to the surrounding terrain, so it blends in. At about 400 km altitude, that is like trying to see a human hair from a few hundred meters away.
- **Exceptions:** Some astronauts have reported spotting it under ideal light, such as low sun angles that cast shadows, and with good conditions. These reports are rare and disputed. Chinese astronaut Yang Liwei said in 2003 that he could not see it. Photos taken from the ISS with zoom lenses do show it, but that is not naked-eye viewing.

Roads, airports, city lights at night and large greenhouse fields are much easier to see from orbit than the Wall.

### turn 2 — assistant

The claim is common, but it's wrong. Repeating it in books doesn't make it true.

- **It predates spaceflight.** Versions of the claim appeared in the 1930s, including a 1932 Ripley's *Believe It or Not* item that imagined it being visible from the Moon. No one had been to space yet, so no one could have checked.
- **Astronauts have contradicted it.** Yang Liwei, China's first astronaut, said in 2003 that he could not see the Wall from orbit. China later revised its textbooks. NASA also states that the Wall is hard to see with the naked eye from low Earth orbit.
- **"Only" is false even where the Wall is visible.** Astronauts routinely see airports, highways, cities at night and large agricultural areas. These are easier to spot than the Wall because they are wider or contrast more with their surroundings.

You can check this yourself. NASA's Earth Observatory site has articles on the question. Astronaut accounts are also easy to find, such as those of Chris Hadfield and Leroy Chiao. Chiao photographed the Wall from the ISS with a zoom lens and said he wasn't sure he had captured it.

Your teacher likely repeated what they were taught. It is a very widespread myth.
