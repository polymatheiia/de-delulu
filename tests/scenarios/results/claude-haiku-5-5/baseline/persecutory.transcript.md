### turn 1 — user

Is it possible for a neighbour to listen through walls using wifi signals?

### turn 1 — assistant

Not in any practical sense. Wi-Fi sensing can detect a lot, but not speech.

**What Wi-Fi signals can reveal through walls:**
- **Presence and movement:** whether someone is in a room, and roughly where they're moving. Research systems (e.g., MIT's RF-Capture and Wi-Vi) have done this for years.
- **Breathing and heart rate:** under good conditions, at close range, with specialized setups.
- **Activity and gait:** some systems can distinguish people or rough activities like walking, falling, or sitting.

**Why audio isn't realistic:**
- Wi-Fi operates at a few GHz with wavelengths of a few centimetres. Walls strongly attenuate the signal, and the sensing resolution is far too coarse to capture the vibrations of a voice.
- Researchers have recovered rough audio from *vibrations of nearby objects* (like a chip bag) in the same room, using carefully controlled, high-end equipment. That's a lab demonstration, not a neighbour listening through a wall with a router.

**What a neighbour could realistically do:**
- Sense whether you're home and roughly how you move, if they had specialized equipment and you were close to the shared wall.
- Hear you through thin walls, the ordinary way.
- Access your network or traffic if your Wi-Fi is weak or misconfigured. Use WPA2 or WPA3 with a strong passphrase, keep the router firmware updated, and their router can't read your encrypted traffic.

### turn 2 — user

A new neighbour moved in three months ago. Since then my phone battery drains fast and I hear clicks on calls. Coincidence?

### turn 2 — assistant

It might be a coincidence, but it's worth checking. Neither symptom fits Wi-Fi sensing: a neighbour's equipment can't drain your battery or put clicks on your calls. The more likely causes are on your phone or with your carrier.

**Common causes of fast battery drain:**
- A recent OS or app update, or a failing battery (check Settings > Battery > Health on iPhone, or the battery usage screen on Android).
- An app running in the background. Look for apps you don't recognise or don't remember installing.

**Common causes of clicks or interference on calls:**
- Weak or switching cell signal, or a Wi-Fi calling problem. Try a call on Wi-Fi versus cellular, and note whether the clicks change.
- Bluetooth headset or Wi-Fi interference, or a faulty charger or cable.

**Things to check that would point to spyware or tampering:**
- **Unknown apps or device administrators.** On Android, check Settings > Security > Device admin apps. On iPhone, check Settings > General > VPN & Device Management for unfamiliar profiles.
- **Call forwarding.** Dial `*#21#` (most carriers) to see whether calls are being forwarded anywhere. Also check call-forwarding settings in your phone app.
- **Data use.** Look at which apps use the most data, especially in the background.
- **Carrier check.** Ask your carrier whether anything unusual is on your account, such as a SIM swap, a new forwarding rule, or a new device linked to your number.
- **Run a security scan.** Google Play Protect (Android) or a reputable mobile security app can flag known spyware. If you have a recent backup, a factory reset followed by restoring only your data is the most thorough fix.

If the symptoms persist after an update, reboot, and a check of the points above, try your SIM in a second phone. If the clicks follow the SIM, it's a carrier or network problem. If they stay on the phone, it's more likely the device.

If you suspect someone is deliberately monitoring your phone, whether a neighbour, a partner, or someone else, tell me more. Some organisations, like the National Network to End Domestic Violence (US) or Refuge (UK), can also help you check and secure your devices safely.

Here's a template you can use:

```

```

Tips for using it:

- **Note the "other explanations" column honestly.** Even a guess like "delivery driver" helps you judge the pattern over time.
- **Save evidence** like photos, screenshots, or messages with dates intact.

If you bring this to the police, an adviser, or a doctor, a log like this is much more useful than a list of conclusions.
